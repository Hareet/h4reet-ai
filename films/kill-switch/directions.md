# A $5-a-day kill switch: three directions

Product read: an AI twin built in the open, by an SRE turned applied-AI lead. Precise, calm, accessible, technical, new: an engineering notebook, not a launch reel. Message shape: mechanism (here is why it can't overspend), with evidence (the breach test's 52 of 52). Brand: the resume's paper, ink, green `.ai`, crimson; Overpass for display, Literata for prose, IBM Plex for the system's own words.

Every direction is 75–90 s, 16:9, narrated, and carries the same spine: the resume line → the meter climbing → the trip at $5.00 → what the visitor sees → how only a human restores it → the walls around it (the tunnel, the agents) → back to the resume line.

## A · Blueprint on the resume's paper

```
A · Blueprint     language: system-diagram (+ draw-on lines, a knife switch, a meter bar)   look: the resume's white paper, a faint 48 px drafting grid, ink lines, Overpass and Plex Mono labels
Palette:          ground #ffffff→#e9eef0 80% · ink #17232e 10% · muted #5b6973 · rule #d6dee2 · green #1c6b54 (live paths, normal) · crimson #ad2549 (the trip, 3%) — from: resume/source/twin.html :root
Logline:          The resume's small-caps line unfolds into a drawing of the stack; calls flow, the meter fills to $3.50 (a warning) and $5.00 (the switch throws itself open); the drawing redraws around the visitor's one line, and closes back on the resume.
Why it fits:      mechanism shape + precise/technical/calm → a diagram that explains by changing; the resume's own paper makes the link from resume to film seamless.
Feel:             continuous, one drawing the camera travels over, 100 BPM, 2–3 events a bar, a burst of ticking calls at 35–45 %, a one-beat stop before the switch throws; with the sound off the first 3 s show the resume line lifting off the page and drawing the first wire.
Risk:             a diagram can read as a slide; it must keep moving (wires drawing, the meter, camera pushes), and the agents' half needs its own drawing.
```

```

Palette:          ground #0f1820→#0b131a 80% · glass #47a386 at 10–20% · text #eef3f5 · muted #93a4ae · crimson #d0587a (the state slab, the stop) — from: resume/source/twin.html (green, green-bar, crimson-bar), the ground the resume's ink deepened
Logline:          The camera falls with a visitor's question through the room, the edge and the gateway; each layer says what it guards; the meter fills in the state slab at the bottom until it turns crimson and the next question stops dead on the gateway; the camera cranes out to the whole stack, walled off from the internet by its one tunnel.
Why it fits:      a layered system worth separating → depth shows "the gateway checks the state first" as a place.
Feel:             continuous, one camera through one world, 100 BPM, a crane at the trip and a pull-out to the whole system at the end; the first 3 s: the beam already falling through the room's slab.
Risk:             depth can become decoration; every camera move must follow the request, and the slabs must stay legible at the angle.
```

Recommendation: **B** for this film. The kill switch's strongest claim is that it was proven, and B is the only one made of the proof itself. A's drawing language is the better spine for the series' calmer films (cost meter, data flows), and C suits "Local models first" (the whole stack as layers).
