The whole production stack runs on one laptop, with one command.

Make up starts fifteen containers: the edge, the gateway, the site, the meter's database, the model server, and the tools that watch them, from metrics and logs to Grafana and Langfuse.

The model server is llama dot cpp on the laptop's GPU, running Qwen3, thirty billion parameters, in twenty-one of the card's twenty-four gigabytes.

Every question tries it first. A local answer costs nothing, and nothing leaves the machine.

Only when it's down does the capped paid fallback answer. On the cloud server, which has no GPU, the Anthropic API answers first.

This is production, not a demo: every image pinned by its digest, and one build at one commit, the same image everywhere.

Offline-first isn't new for Hareet. At Medic, the community health workers using the Community Health Toolkit had older Android phones, and mostly no network.

So Hareet designed Voice for CHT: the health worker speaks the visit, and the form fills itself, offline. Hareet helped the team fine-tune its models to two hundred seventy-three megabytes, to run beside the app on a four-gigabyte phone. No audio or transcript leaves the phone as telemetry.
