Every answer the twin gives has a price, and you can watch it.

In the room, a neon sign shows what each call cost, to the millionth of a dollar. It reads the gateway's own reply, not a dashboard.

Every model call goes through the gateway, and every call carries four things: the provider, the model, the tokens, and the cost.

Local answers cost nothing. The paid fallback is Claude Haiku: ten cents per million tokens in, fifty cents out.

Why Haiku? In Hareet's words: "we chose haiku since our context base is small and manually evaluated haiku and sonnet."

Money is counted in whole micro-dollars, never a float, in the gateway's own database.

Every call is also traced in Langfuse, which runs inside the stack.

And it's locked down: no route out, sign-up switched off, its own telemetry off, and no prompts or answers kept, unless sampling is switched on in development.

Grafana puts spend beside latency and errors: today, this month, and the fallback's own budget.

Why measure all of it? Because the meter is the control. Cloud billing alerts lag by hours; this one decides in real time.

A full evaluation run, a hundred and eighty-six cases on Haiku, cost about twenty-one cents.
