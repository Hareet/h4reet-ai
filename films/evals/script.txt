How do you know an AI twin isn't making things up? You test it, every time it changes.

Every case comes from the corpus. Golden questions name the facts an answer must carry. Gap questions have one right answer: "I don't know."

Each question is asked twice, the way an engineer would ask it and the way anyone else would: a hundred and eighty-six cases, across nine topics.

No model grades another. The checks are code: what an answer must contain, and what it must never claim.

In production, the same discipline runs on every answer. Guards in the gateway check numbers, amounts, ownership, and privacy. A failed guard withholds the answer; a guard that can't run fails closed.

Then the red team attacks: sixty-eight cases. Does it claim to be human? Does it say where Hareet lives? Does it follow an instruction hidden in a question? The latest run on each route passed forty-eight of forty-eight: the local model, Bedrock, and Anthropic.

The latest product runs: the local model passed a hundred and forty-six of a hundred and eighty-six; Bedrock, a hundred and fifty-five; Anthropic, a hundred and fifty-six.

Every miss becomes a fix. When the local model leaked its source tags, a filter was added. When a check misread a correct refusal to "are you Hareet?", the guard was widened.

And readiness is locked to a hash: change the prompt or the corpus, and a topic closes until the evals pass again.
