## 04 Hobbies and travel
### His words
- Q: How did snowboarding start for you?
  A: "I moved to Colorado about 15 years ago, and met my now-wife here, who had recently moved back to her hometown. We spent 10 years traveling through Sub-Saharan Africa and Asia setting up work projects, which I'll tell you about related to Medic. Whenever we arrived back home, we always fell in love with our state of Colorado, the most gorgeous state in the Union. More recently, we decided to take up Winter Sports and got back into Snowboarding. With the help of a neighbor, he was able to provide me with all the necessary gear to get going. We live in a city house in Denver, so I throw on a snowboard bag and ride a bicycle to the train station where I can board with all my equipment and be dropped directly off at Winter Park Resort and ski lifts. It's a great experience and I think the only resort in North America accessible via train. You can often find me there, snowboarding through the trees, enjoying the back side of the mountain. We hope to gear up and travel for a destination snowboard trip soon, eagerly awaiting a chance to snowboard in Japan. If you are local to Colorado, we are Ikon Pass people, simply due to the better public transit offerings to their mountains."
- Q: You said you got back into snowboarding. When did you first learn?
  A: "As a child, but I never took lessons or had the proper clothing, so I never enjoyed it or got beyond the very basics. Recently, as an adult, I decided to self-teach myself with YouTube and iteration. Now I'm successfully going down double black diamond slopes."
- Q: What do you love most about snowboarding now?
  A: "Enjoying riding through the trees on a fresh snowy day."
- Q: Now the bicycle on the wall. What kind of riding do you do?
  A: "Commuter riding around the city. I ride to my parents' house to have lunch weekly, I also ride a one-wheel and love exploring the extended city neighborhoods on a bicycle."
- Q: What do you love about getting around the city by bike?
  A: "The weather is usually sunny and gorgeous, and biking allows you to take more of the outside in. I don't like driving at all, and only drive if I'm going hiking in the mountains and taking my dog. I even enjoy winter biking and layering up in clothes to keep warm and enjoying the crisp air."
- Q: The sign in the room says "my dog thinks i'm cool." Tell me about your dog.
  A: "Arya is a Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever, known as a Duck Toller. When I used to stay in Minneapolis, one of my colleagues invited me up to a cabin at Lake of the Woods, which is a State Park at the border of Minnesota and Canada. My first time going up to a cabin, I thought we were camping outside in the cold and brought a ton of appropriate equipment. Instead, the cabin was essentially a second home, full of furnishings, isolated deep in Lake of the Woods. One of the neighbors during that stay was on a hunting trip, and had 3 duck tollers with him. I ended up playing with the dogs for the entire time we were there, that my friend offered me the keys to the cabin and told me I should come back and grab a dog or puppy from the neighbor. Within weeks, I had brought Arya back home and my life forever changed. She has kept me outside, active, walking, and playing when most of my hobbies and work life revolved around computers or tinkering with hardware. She introduced me to most of my friends, and especially my wife. Arya is now 13 years old, and has went camping across the country and across Canada several times with me. She's always up for an adventure, but certainly enjoys a snooze in her senior years. <3"
- Q: Of all your travels, which trip has stayed with you the most?
  A: "Traveling for work has elevated the relationships, connections, and my love for the places visited to another degree. Most recently, I was in Senegal to visit my CEO and discuss our plan for offline voice-to-CHT models, as I was traveling to Nigeria afterwards to discuss with the partner on our implementation. In Lagos, I also visited Fela Kuti's Shrine and got to see local music at the same sites that Wizkid, Burna Boy and others grew up and made famous. The hospitality, friendliness, energy, and AI-for-good projects, will always have a place in my heart."
- Q: What have all those years of traveling for work taught you?
  A: "That's tough to answer. It's taught me how markets, systems, humans, economics, global aid, support all work and coincide together. It's what has kept me motivated to help projects with a human-centered approach."

## 05 Mission
### His words
- Q: What do you want your work to do for people?

- Q: How do you decide whether a new technology is worth bringing to new users?
  A: "Taking into consideration the effectiveness of using deterministic approaches, and how cost effective the evaluation, resource-usage, and maintenance is on AI solutions. I spent time architecting AI solutions that could run on their phones, which we labeled as Runtime AI in our Medic AI strategy that I co-authored with our CEO. I was also the winner of AWS Imagine 2025 Momentum to Modernize Award, where I implemented Smart Task Prioritization inside the CHT. A different focus around AI in our strategy was Buildtime AI, and my focus was to pull engineering dreams off the shelf, that were once unattainable or too time consuming, and detail out the implementation path towards them. For Medic, this meant allowing non-technical users the ability to participate in the creation of new features and maintainability of the software. I architected and built the cht-agent, a multi-agent you can deploy, install with all the considerations an SRE brings: cost-effectiveness, model agnostic, locally-owned, context management, curated domain knowledge-corpus."
