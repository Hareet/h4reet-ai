# ADR 0056: No backdrop blur: the HUD draws no `backdrop-filter` at any quality level, in any layout or view; over the room each text is legible from its own plate, at WCAG AA over the room's brightest and darkest pixels, as spec 029 tested it; the right column stands as two halves with an opaque grey edge (spec 030)

## Context

> 1. You can see from one of our latest two screenshots in the resume/ that the right side panel UI is not transparent, it is more blurred. We also want to change the UI buttons to look more like the screenshot in resume/ (the other one that is the latest). Let's separate the right-side panel UI into two halves, making the separation a horizontal slice. Then use the buttons, and let's create them in css and data so we can swap them out. 2) Let's make the character selection screen, the credits, the data flow all dark themed web pages, and use the same button styling if appropriate on the character selection screen. Maybe use the studio pipeline to create the buttons for UI? https://www.sprungstudios.com/how-to-design-a-ui-button-for-video-games/

Night 4's request, in the words spec 030 quotes from it (spec.md:55, Q2's reading): "an opaque or transparent background". His whole review of 2026-11-15, which those words come from, is quoted in spec 029's spec.md:19.

Neither screenshot was read by any session of spec 030 (`resume/` isn't in the worktrees; spec.md:17). The orders describe the first one in their own words, not his (spec.md:31).

What drew the blur, at the base:

- **Spec 005's FR-025** (`specs/005-full-window-room/spec.md:294`): "A backdrop blur SHALL be drawn behind the panels at quality levels 0 and 1 only. The blur SHALL be decoration: FR-024 never relies on it, and FR-006 turns it off." **ADR 0043's decision 5** (`0043-the-room-fills-the-window.md:25`) records it: "a blur only at quality levels 0 and 1; opaque panels under reduced transparency, more contrast, forced colours, or no backdrop blur."
- The code: `room.css`'s `--hud-blur: blur(12px)` (`:109`) and `html[data-blur="on"] .hud-glass` (`:678-681`); the split layout's `[data-blur="on"]` rule for the list and the bar (`room/hud/split.css:79-82`, spec 018's); the root's `data-blur`, set by `layout.ts`'s `setBlur` (`:121`, `:485-487`) from `app.ts`'s `blurAt` (`:210`, `level <= 1`) and its two calls (`:295`, `:1149`); and the fallback `@supports not ((backdrop-filter: blur(1px)) or (-webkit-backdrop-filter: blur(1px)))` (`room.css:189-200`), which made the glass opaque where a browser couldn't blur.
- **Spec 029** (night 4) made each part over the room translucent and put each run of its words on a plate (its FR-001 to FR-004, `specs/029-room-polish/spec.md:182-185`; `--over-glass-alpha: 0.3`, `--over-plate-alpha: 0.5`), and kept the blur: its FR-002 says "the blur (`data-blur`) applies as today" (`:183`).

Spec 030's Q1 reads his "not transparent, it is more blurred" as naming the backdrop blur, not the tint (spec.md:39, :54). That is spec 030's reading, beside his words, not his words.

## Decision

1. **No backdrop blur** (spec 030's FR-001; Q1, option A, taken under his rule 1): no rule of `room.css`, `room/hud/split.css`, `talk/talk.css`, or `bubble.css`, at the top level or inside an at-rule, sets `backdrop-filter` or `-webkit-backdrop-filter` to anything but `none`, at any quality level, in any layout (split, column, dock, stacked), at rest or in the walking view. `--hud-blur`, `layout.setBlur`, `app.ts`'s `blurAt`, and the root's `data-blur` are gone (A's tip: no match for any of them under `src/room/`, `src/talk/`; plan §R-A1). Spec 005's quality levels keep their other effects.
2. **Legibility from each text's own plate** (spec 030's FR-005, FR-006; Q2): over the room, the glass is spec 029's translucent glass and every run of words sits on its own plate, so the contrast lives in the plate and the glass beneath it, never in a blur. Every text reaches 4.5:1 (3:1 large) and every border, icon, and focus ring 3:1, against what lies beneath composited over pure white and over pure black, the room's brightest and darkest pixels, computed from the stylesheets by the cascade, as spec 029's `test/ui/hud-over-layers.test.ts` computes it. Spec 029's alphas are kept (A's tip, `room.css:149-150`): every pair of both halves holds at 0.3 and 0.5 with no blur (plan §R-A3, "FR-005's rule wasn't needed").
3. **The right column as two halves, over the room in every layout and view** (spec 030's FR-002 to FR-005; Q2 to Q4, Q85): a top half (in the split layout the list: "Back", "In the room", the list's heading, and the entries; in the column layout the column; in the dock the dock's sheet) and a bottom half (the controls: in the split layout the bar's tiles, the walking control, and "Eat"; in the column layout and the dock the bar), split by a horizontal slice drawn as nothing (`0.75rem × --wall-scale-right` in the split layout, `0.75rem` in the column layout and the dock). Both halves are marked `data-over="room"` always, at rest too, so both take spec 029's over-room look of decision 2 wherever they stand. The conversation panel on the left of the split layout keeps spec 029's rule (over the room only); in the column layout and the dock it sits inside the top half and takes its look.
4. **Opaque where the visitor asks** (spec 030's FR-007; spec 005's FR-006, spec 029's FR-004, kept): under `prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce` or `prefers-contrast: more` both alphas are 1 (A's tip, `room.css:1279-1284`, the same rule also under forced colours); under forced colours both halves are `Canvas` with a `CanvasText` edge: the top half's ground by the glass's own rule (`room.css:1124-1133`, `.hud-glass`; in the split layout `room/hud/split.css:514-518`, the list) and its edge by `room.css:1400-1403` (the column layout and the dock) and `room/hud/split.css:596-600` (the split layout's list); the bottom half's ground and edge by `room.css:1394-1398` (`.hud-half`).

The blur SHALL be decoration: FR-024 never relies on it, and FR-006 turns it off." | No backdrop blur at any level. Its first sentence (the dark glass, "at the lowest opacity, in steps of 5%, at which every pair of FR-024 passes") stands for panels beside the room; over the room spec 029's FR-002 and FR-003 had already put its glass and plates in its place, and spec 030's FR-005 puts the right column's halves there in every layout and view |

Spec 030's Amendments table (spec.md:248) also names, for the same change: spec 005's FR-058 ("with the HUD and its blur shown"; its "the blur going at level 2" has nothing left to go), FR-006 (its blur fallbacks; the opaque fallbacks kept); spec 018's FR-014 (the split layout's 0.78 glass and, at `split.css:79-82`, its blur); and spec 029's FR-002 ("the blur (`data-blur`) applies as today") (spec.md:249, :251). ## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the blur** (spec 005's FR-025 as written, at levels 0 and 1): his words name it as the fault: "the right side panel UI is not transparent, it is more blurred". A blur over the room smears it whatever the tint, and FR-025 made it decoration ("FR-024 never relies on it"), so removing it changes no contrast (spec 030's Q1, spec.md:54).
- **A lighter blur** (Q1's option C, 4 px): the room is still smeared behind the column, only less; the quality-level switch, `data-blur`, and the `@supports` fallback would all stay, for a look his words ask to remove.
- **No blur only where spec 029 marks a part over the room** (Q1's option B; with it Q2's option B, the column at spec 018's 0.78 glass less the blur beside the wall): spec 029's geometry marks nothing of the split layout's right column over the room at the room's framing (its FR-005), while the orders' description puts the column over the room at a wide window (spec.md:31, :41); a column clear everywhere holds under both readings.
- **An opaque panel**: legible with no plates, and kept as the fallback where the visitor asks for it (decision 4). As the default it covers the room behind the whole column. Tonight his words name "the right side panel UI" "not transparent" (that this panel is the right column is spec 030's reading). On night 4 he asked "i think we could make the interface have an opaque or transparent background, so that it way in those conditions the interface won't block the room visibility" (his words, verbatim, spec 029's spec.md:19); spec 029 took the translucent reading of those words (its Q1, `specs/029-room-polish/spec.md:54`).

## Consequences

- **The halves' edge is an opaque grey, `--half-border: #8a8f98`** (A's tip, `room.css:156`; drawn at `room.css:1369`, `:1382`, `:1389` and `room/hud/split.css:586-589`), not the tiles' translucent `--tile-border`, which against a white room beside the 0.3 glass reaches about 1.7:1, under FR-006's 3:1 (the page's Q84, spec 030's Q84, spec.md:98; plan §R-A3). A grey whose luminance lies between 3:1 under white and 3:1 over black reaches 3:1 against white or black outside it, whatever the glass inside. The page's Q92 records what it reaches: 3.25:1 against a white room outside, 1.64:1 against its own glass inside over that white room (6.46 and 6.24 over black); its recommended answer, taken: one side is enough, the edge marking where the panel ends against the room, as spec 029's model and WCAG 1.4.11's boundary read it. The model reads it so: a border against the colour inside or outside, whichever it stands out from more (A's tip, `test/helpers/layers.ts:456-466`). C's dark pages use the same grey for their tables' borders.
- **The edge lies inside each half's padding** (the page's Q99, its recommended answer, taken, as `rework-030a` built it for `rv-030a`'s R-2): the split layout's list pads `calc(0.5rem - 1px) calc(0.75rem - 1px)` (A's tip, `room/hud/split.css:586-589`), and the column layout's and the dock's top half `calc(0.25rem - 1px)` (`room.css:1380-1391`), so each content box is what it was before the edge, in height (the "Dog" ratchet's model) and in width.
- **No `@supports` fallback, since nothing blurs** (spec 030's T-A07; A's commits `229b45c3` RED, `801b619a` GREEN): the block at the base's `room.css:189-200` is gone, and no rule asks whether the browser has a backdrop filter. The `.hud-glass { backdrop-filter: none }` resets left in the media fallbacks (A's tip, `room.css:175-176`, `:187-188`, `:1131-1132`) are redundant and harmless; T-INT may fold them (plan §R-A1).
- **The tests that hold it**, at A's tip:
  - `test/ui/hud-no-blur.test.ts`: every sheet's declarations read (`:62-91`), no `backdrop-filter` but `none`, no `--hud-blur`, no selector naming `data-blur`, no `@supports` naming a backdrop filter (`:83-90`); through the real room stepped from quality level 0 to 3, no `data-blur` on the root, and no `setBlur` (`:93-123`).
  - `test/ui/hud-halves.test.ts`: the two halves and the slice, through the real room at 1366 × 768, 1440 × 900, 1920 × 1080 (split), 1280 × 720 (column), 390 × 844 (dock), at rest and walking in 3D and two of them in 2D, checked against numbers the test derives itself (`:133`); none in the stacked layout (`:280`). `test/room/halves.test.ts`: `halves()` on hand-written boxes.
  - `test/ui/hud-halves-contrast.test.ts`: every pair of both halves, the bottom half's own edge among them (`:89-93`), at AA over white and black at those windows and views (`:95`); opaque under the visitor's asks (`:120-136`), and `Canvas` under forced colours, a case that reads the bottom half's rule alone (`:138-143`).
  - Spec 029's `test/ui/hud-over-layers.test.ts` and `test/ui/hud-over-contrast.test.ts`, whose model (`test/helpers/layers.ts`, extracted by T-A01) the halves' test reuses.
- **The halves cost room** (spec 030's Q81, Q82): the split list is `12 × scale − 4` CSS px shorter, so "Dog" shows whole at 1,558 of 3,329 split stages where 1,601 held it, the list scrolling in its panel on the rest; the dock's slice takes 12 px from the phone's stage (384 × 422 becomes 384 × 410; the 320 × 568 screen's 320 × 250 becomes 320 × 238, still at the 2D drawing's scale 1 and over spec 004's 304 × 196).
- **Less work per frame**: one fewer compositing pass per panel at quality levels 0 and 1, so spec 005's FR-058 frame-rate floor holds with less to draw.
- **Lookdev keeps its own `data-blur`** on its own page's root (A's tip, `src/lookdev/stage.ts:435-462`): it isn't the HUD, and no sheet of the room reads it.
- **Cost: $0** in the cloud and in model spend; no byte leaves the visitor's device that didn't before (spec 030's Data-Flow Disclosure Entries).
- **If he overturns Q1 to Q4** on the morning page, group A is reworked before he merges (spec 030's Assumptions), and this ADR with it.
