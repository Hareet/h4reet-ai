## Context

Intent 000 promises "an animated AI twin of me" and "four pre-generated avatars", in a room "whose art direction is inspired by GTA V: stylized illustrative realism, saturated light, cinematic framing. Inspired-by only — no Rockstar assets, logos, typefaces, or character likenesses; everything is original." Intent 003 asks for this ADR before the studio builds its workflow, "so nothing is made twice", and records where the survey of 2026-10-12 leaned:

> "Claude's survey of 2026-10-12 leans toward the convention three.js's TalkingHead library expects (glTF, a Mixamo-compatible skeleton, ARKit's 52 blendshapes, and the 15 Oculus visemes), because VRM's five vowels have no closed-lip shapes."

The survey itself is a private page, outside this repository (the intent's merge-request note). This draft rests on what the intent, spec 003, and its research quote from it. Spec 003's budget defaults are the frame: a first visit of at most 10 MB of assets in Phase 2 and 25 MB with the room, a 30 fps median on the reference phone, and a step down below 24 fps for 5 seconds (SC-006, FR-024).

## Decision

- **A real-time 3D rig, not generated video.** The twin and the visitor avatars are glTF models animated in the browser (three.js), so the twin can answer any message and look the same in every frame; a generated video would need a model call per answer and couldn't follow the conversation.
- **The format:** binary glTF 2.0 (`.glb`), one file per model or animation, with its textures embedded as PNG, JPEG, or WebP (`EXT_texture_webp`), as the arrival check admits (research R-02). No Draco, meshopt, or KTX2 until an amendment adds them and the check grows to read them.
- **The skeletons:**
  - the twin and the two human visitor avatars: a Mixamo-compatible humanoid skeleton, its bones named as Mixamo names them (`mixamorig:Hips`, `mixamorig:Spine`, and so on), so one set of humanoid animations drives all three;
  - the dog and the dinosaur: a named rig each (`dog`, `dinosaur`), with their bones listed in the rig's spec when the characters are made; their animations live under `assets/animations/dog/` and `assets/animations/dinosaur/`.
- **The face, for the twin only:** ARKit's 52 blendshapes and the 15 Oculus visemes (`viseme_sil`, `viseme_PP`, `viseme_FF`, `viseme_TH`, `viseme_DD`, `viseme_kk`, `viseme_CH`, `viseme_SS`, `viseme_nn`, `viseme_RR`, `viseme_aa`, `viseme_E`, `viseme_I`, `viseme_O`, `viseme_U`) as morph targets, named in `mesh.extras.targetNames`, the one `extras` the arrival check keeps. Visitors don't speak through their avatars, so theirs need body animation only (spec 003, Assumptions).
- **The first set** (spec 003, Assumptions): the twin with idle, talking, listening, and greeting animations and a still image for the 2D mode; the four placeholder visitors ("a generic woman, a generic man, a generic dinosaur, a generic dog", intent 000), each with an idle animation and a preview image.
- **Per-asset caps, by kind,** as the Author set them on `main` when he accepted this ADR (1f929db0), and as his f21eff08 then raised the avatar's:
  - `avatar`, the twin's model and each visitor's: at most 21,000,000 bytes, 180,000 triangles, and 46,000,000 bytes of texture memory (f21eff08). 1f929db0 set them at 6,000,000 bytes, 90,000 triangles, and 33,554,432 bytes, and this text read those until this amendment. f21eff08's subject names the twin; `caps.json` holds one `avatar` block, so its caps hold every avatar, the twin's model and each visitor's;
  - `animation`: at most 1,000,000 bytes (1f929db0, unchanged);
  - `still` and `preview`: at most 300,000 bytes each (1f929db0, unchanged).

  In `caps.json` they read `"per_asset": {"avatar": {"bytes": 21000000, "triangles": 180000, "texture_bytes": 46000000}, "animation": {"bytes": 1000000}, "still": {"bytes": 300000}, "preview": {"bytes": 300000}}`, beside the room's block below, and the first visit's budgets read `"first_visit_bytes": {"phase2": 10000000, "phase3": 26000000}`. Phase 3's budget read 25,000,000 from the file's first commit on `main` (1ee99100, 2026-10-26, "Apply the guard patch (T003)") until ab4876f5 set it at 26,000,000; phase 2's 10,000,000 hasn't changed. The night's line (the named stack of 2026-11-14) can't merge `main` (Q286), so its own `assets/policy/caps.json` reads phase 3 at 25,000,000 until the Author's merge of `main` into it (Q288, Q286). The arrival check measures each file's bytes, triangles, and texture memory (width × height × 4 × 4/3) and refuses one over its kind's cap (rule F2), and refuses a first visit over its budget whatever the caps (rule F3), counted by transfer size, each file at gzip level 9.

  At these caps, one visitor's first visit is 47,000,000 bytes before the room: the twin's model (21,000,000), its four animations (4,000,000), and a visitor's model (21,000,000) and its idle (1,000,000). That is over phase 2's 10,000,000, which F3 applies only while `assets/room/` holds no file it serves. `main` holds the room's 21 files, 6,593,757 bytes at gzip level 9 with the chalkboard's re-intake, so phase 3's 26,000,000 applies, and that visit is 53,593,757 bytes, 27,593,757 over it. F3 counts more than that visit: every GLB under `assets/animations/` and the largest visitor's files (`first_visit_total` in `tools/gates/arrival_check.py`). The first set at its caps is the twin's model (21,000,000), its eight animations, the twin's four and the four visitors' idles (8,000,000), the largest visitor's model (21,000,000), and the room's files (6,593,757): 56,593,757 bytes, 30,593,757 over phase 3's 26,000,000. F3 would refuse either, so no first visit can have its files at their caps: the budget, not the per-asset caps, bounds what a visit carries.

  Before this amendment, at 1f929db0's caps and phase 3's 25,000,000, these figures read 17,000,000 before the room; 23,593,757 with it, 1,406,243 under phase 3; and 26,593,757 for the first set, 1,593,757 over (593,757 over 26,000,000). The first visit measured with the dog's round-4 files was 25,050,327 bytes, 50,327 over 25,000,000 and 949,673 under 26,000,000 (the page's Q288; the Author's answer, "B for the dog", is ab4876f5).
- **The room's caps** (spec 004, Amendment C7; its FR-052 and FR-053), the Author's own commits on `main` (spec 004's P-6, then ec2fdb87), in `per_asset.room`, which holds exactly these six keys, at FR-053's starting figures but for the two ec2fdb87 raised for the lookdev pieces, and at FR-052's default floor:
  - `gzip_bytes`: 2,500,000, the bytes of a room file at gzip level 9, the bytes served, per file (the arrival check's F2) and per piece (spec 004's compositor);
  - `triangles`: 100,000, per GLB and per piece (FR-053's starting figure is 40,000);
  - `small_props`: the pieces the Author names, a model file's stem being its piece's ID; `main`'s list names none;
  - `small_prop_texture_px`: 1,024, the largest texture width or height on a small prop, a power of two;
  - `scene_texture_bytes`: 200,000,000, the whole scene's texture memory on a phone, width × height × 4 × 4/3 per texture, models, image cards, avatars, and live-face canvases together (FR-053's starting figure is 96,000,000). That is about twice, 2.08 times, what the reference phone was budgeted for: spec 004's SC-006 reads "Texture memory on the reference phone stays at or under 96 MB", and its words haven't changed;
  - `legibility_floor_css_px`: 9, the smallest cap height a readable face may show, in CSS pixels, at the production framing on both reference stages.

  In `caps.json` they read `"room": {"gzip_bytes": 2500000, "triangles": 100000, "small_props": [], "small_prop_texture_px": 1024, "scene_texture_bytes": 200000000, "legibility_floor_css_px": 9}`. The arrival check refuses a room file over its byte or triangle cap, or a small prop's texture over its side (F2), refuses every room file while the block is absent from `main`, and can't check (exit 2) while the block holds another key, or one of the wrong type; its first visit counts every file the room serves under `assets/room/` at gzip level 9, image cards included, never a directory's `records.json` or `LICENSE.md`, with phase 3's budget once that folder holds one, 26,000,000 bytes since ab4876f5 (F3, Amendment C6). The caps are configuration the Author owns: agents never raise one (FR-053).
- **The reference phone and the mobile profile** (research R-13): a 2023 mid-range Android phone, a Samsung Galaxy A54 or the closest the Author has, recorded by model in each measurement; 9 Mbps down and a 150 ms round trip.
- **The capability floor** (the site's check before any 3D download, FR-023): WebGL2; where the browser reports it, `navigator.deviceMemory` of at least 4; and a maximum texture size of at least 4096. A device below it goes straight to the 2D mode.
- **The art direction:** stylized illustrative realism, saturated light, cinematic framing, as intent 000 puts it, and everything original: "no Rockstar assets, logos, typefaces, or character likenesses". The twin is a stylized likeness of the Author, made from the Author's photos; the visitors are original characters that resemble no real person (FR-017). `REVIEW.md`'s "Arriving assets" pass checks it on arrival.

## Alternatives considered

- **VRM** (VRoid's glTF profile): a ready standard for avatars, but its five vowel expressions have no closed-lip shapes for P, B, and M, which lip-sync needs (the survey's reason above).
- **Generated video** of the twin: photoreal, but a model call per answer, a lag the conversation can't hide, and nothing to fall back to when the local tier is off.
- **Compressed geometry and KTX2 textures now:** smaller downloads on phones, but the arrival check would need their decoders to see inside them (research R-02). An amendment can add them with the check's readers.

## Consequences

- The studio builds its workflow toward one target: these skeletons, these shapes, these caps.
- Until the Author accepted this ADR, no avatar or animation could enter the repository (FR-018); stills, previews, and the evaluation's published images could. Since 1f929db0, avatars and animations can, each held to the caps (F2) and the first visit (F3).
- The arrival check enforces the caps (F2) and the first visit's total (F3). Checking each file against the skeleton's bone names and the twin's 67 shapes is a follow-up now that this is accepted, as FR-018's "the skeleton, and, for the twin, the facial shapes" needs. No arrival-check rule or spec 003 task holds it yet, and `REVIEW.md`'s "Arriving assets" pass names no such item.
- The caps are first estimates. The measurements on the reference phone (spec 003's waiting group, SC-005, SC-006) may tighten them, never loosen SC-006's budget. The caps and the budgets are the Author's configuration: phase 3's 26,000,000 (ab4876f5) is 1,000,000 bytes over SC-006's "at most 25 MB once the room's assets arrive in Phase 3", whose words haven't changed; spec 020's Assumptions name the raise as a deviation from spec 009 FR-038's first-visit budget (verify4-020, Q288).
- The room's files waited for the room's caps on `main`, and for the arrival check's spec 004 amendments (P-5), which `make test` holds before the first file under `assets/room/` (spec 004, FR-077); `main` now holds both, and the room's 21 files. Spec 004's lookdev records the look (FR-070); the Author accepted this ADR first, so ADR 0037 amends it with the look instead.
