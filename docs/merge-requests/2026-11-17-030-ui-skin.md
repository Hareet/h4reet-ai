- **`.claude/hooks/bash-gate.sh`, as built**: after the old rule (`:447-448`, unchanged), a rule that reads `rm` where
  a command starts, as its `rm_start` lists them: a line's start; after `;`, `&`, `|`, or `!`; after a subshell's or a
  group's `(` or `{`, a case arm's `)`, a backtick, or `$(`; after the keywords `if`, `elif`, `then`, `do`, `else`,
  `while`, `until`, and `coproc`; after a wrapper in its list (`exec`, `sudo`, `doas`, `env`, `nohup`, `time`,
  `command`, `builtin`, `xargs`, `timeout`, `nice`, `ionice`, `stdbuf`, `setsid`, `watch`, `busybox`, a shell and its
  `-c`, `source`, `eval`) or `find -exec` and `-execdir`; past the rule's own skips (`rm_skip`: an option and one
  argument, quoted or not; a duration; an assignment, with `=` or `+=`, its value quoted or not); by path, quoted, or
  after a backslash; in each of `has()`'s three readings. Its simple command runs to the first `;`, `&` or `|` that
  isn't a redirection's (`2>&1`, `>&2`, `2>&-`, `<&0`, `&>`, `>|`). When that cut leaves a quote open or ends on a
  backslash, it runs on to the line's end, and over each newline a quote holds open, so a quoted or escaped separator
  (`';'`, `'|'`, `"&"`, `"a;b"`, `\;`) or a quoted newline doesn't end it. It is recursive when a word is `-r`, `-R`, a
  cluster holding either, or `--recursive` (or an abbreviation), before or after the operands. It is refused when a
  word holds `$` or a backtick (`$VAR`, `${…}`, `$(…)`, `` `…` ``), since the gate can't prove such a path is inside
  the workspace, or when an operand starts with `/`, `~`, or `..`, the old rule's literal targets in the spellings it
  misses (Q115). A redirection's target isn't an operand, but an escaped `\>` or a quoted `'<'` is. Any `$` in what it
  reads counts, so these fail closed, refused, each pinned by a `block` row: a `$` in a redirection
  (`rm -rf build 2>"$LOG"`) or in a trailing comment (`rm -rf build # $x`), `rv-handover-030`'s nit 5; and a `$` later
