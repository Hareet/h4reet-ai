## What changed and why

User Story 2's room and User Story 5's access to it, on the room's floor: everything is static, every object and surface answers with the spec's fixed messages, and nothing calls a model or sends a byte beyond the site's own files.

1. **The choice (T040; FR-001–FR-004).** `src/choice/`: the path ("Engineer", "Not an engineer") and the avatar, intent 000's four placeholders ("Woman", "Man", "Dinosaur", "Dog", each a flat shape with its name), as radio buttons in two fieldsets. "Enter" stays disabled until both are chosen, with the hint "Choose a path and an avatar to enter.". Beside them are "Download the resume (PDF)", which works without entering, and "How data flows". The visit (`visit.ts`, data-model.md §9) lives only in `startRoom`'s memory. The form is built by script and never submits, so no browser restores its choices on reload.
2. **The AI disclosure (FR-004).** It stays `index.html`'s header, now sticky and opaque on every screen, with `scroll-padding-top` so a focused control never scrolls under it (WCAG 2.4.11). contracts/room-scene.md §1 lists it in the toolbar; it is "always visible" either way, and a screen reader meets it once.
3. **The scene (T041; FR-005; R-11).** `src/room/scene.ts`, Three.js, all placeholder geometry: the desk with its laptop and screen, the snowboard hanging on the left, the bicycle on the right, the small window with artwork above it and its view of a fence and a tree, the twin's figure at the desk, and the visitor's chosen avatar as a flat cut-out in front. Each piece carries a label ending "(placeholder)", and the stage says "Placeholder shapes stand in for the room's real objects." Each object has an invisible hit box for raycasts. **Its own chunk:** the room imports `scene.ts` only after the WebGL 2 check, as the choice screen appears. If the chunk hasn't arrived 4 s after "Enter" (`SCENE_WAIT_MS`), or its import fails, the room goes to 2D and starts the hand-off, and a late chunk is ignored. The stage says "Loading the room…" meanwhile. Three.js's chunk is about 146 KB compressed, and a device without WebGL 2 never downloads it.
4. **The hand-off (T042; FR-007–FR-009; R-13).** `src/room/handoff.ts`: 4.5 s in 3D (the twin turns and the paper arcs into the visitor's hands), 1 s under reduced motion (a crossfade, nothing moves), and three still frames of 1.5 s in 2D. Its timeline has no way to end early. While it plays, the room's controls are `inert`, every handler also checks the visit's stage, and focus rests on the room's heading. Its line goes to the polite status region, which is in the page from the first screen. At its end, focus moves to "Read the resume now", beside "Keep it for later"; either one opens the room, and "Resume" stays in the toolbar from then on.
5. **Modes and quality (T043; FR-042, FR-048; R-04).** `src/room/quality.ts`:
   - The controller steps quality 0 → 3, one step per 5-second window whose median frame rate is under the target (30 on a touch-primary device, 60 otherwise), and switches to 2D below 24 frames a second at quality 3. It never steps back up.
   - Only a hidden page restarts the window (`visibilitychange`), and no frame counts while hidden. A long gap on a visible page is a slow frame, so a page drawing under 1 frame a second steps down and reaches 2D.
   - Each step lowers the pixel ratio, then shadows, then textures and the idle sway. Level 3 redraws the labels, the placeholders' only textures, at half resolution.
   - The WebGL 2 check uses `failIfMajorPerformanceCaveat`, so a software renderer starts in 2D. A lost context gets one restore within 3 s.
   - `src/room/flat.ts` is the 2D mode: an inline SVG of the same placeholders, every piece labeled "(placeholder)", with a button over each object that says so too. "2D view" switches at any time, and every road to 2D sends one `mode_2d` and keeps the visit.
6. **The accessible layer and the layout (T044; FR-043–FR-047).** `src/room/a11y.ts` holds the "In the room" list, whose buttons call the same `selectObject` as a raycast hit and a touch, and the status region. `layout.ts` gives the order: toolbar, stage, list, conversation, resume. The canvas and the drawing are `aria-hidden`, and the drawing's buttons are `tabindex="-1"`, so the list is the objects' one accessible equivalent in both modes. `room.css`, `choice.css`, and `talk.css` give every target 44 by 44 CSS pixels and every focusable element a 2-pixel `#0b4f9c` outline. Portrait is the base layout, with the stage's height in `dvh`; landscape puts the stage beside the list and the conversation. `framing.ts` moves the camera back until every piece is in view, from a 0.45 aspect to a wide desktop. Transitions are off under reduced motion.
7. **The conversation surfaces (T045; FR-023, FR-039, FR-040; R-14).** `src/talk/` and `content/messages.yaml` hold the eight messages of data-model.md §7, each written in advance.
   - An object answers `topic_not_ready` with its topic.
   - A typed turn answers `cannot_talk.unavailable`, followed by "Read the resume" and "Download the resume (PDF)". The visitor's text stays in the box, since it went nowhere.
   - The talk button answers `voice_unavailable` and moves focus to the text box, without touching the microphone. A press is one gesture however long it's held: a pointer press owns the click it fires, and a click alone, as a screen reader sends it, is a press of its own.
   - `cannotTalkFor` maps spec 000's degraded causes to the three classes for Phase 7.
8. **The manifest (T046).** `build/manifest.ts` builds `dist/room-manifest.json` (the three objects with `ready: false`, `free_talk.ready: false`, the resume's item IDs, the four avatars, `placeholders: true`, the eight event kinds) and the committed `config/room-events.yaml`, now with the resume's 15 item IDs. `npm run manifest -w apps/web` rewrites the YAML, and `test/manifest.test.ts` fails `make test` while it's stale. The same plugin bundles the messages and the manifest into the page as `virtual:room-content`, so no answer needs a request.
9. **The resume, in the room (T029's view; contracts/room-scene.md §1).** The room owns the resume's panel: its heading "The resume", Escape, focus back to "Resume", and `resume_opened`. `src/room/resume-port.ts`'s `resumeView()` adapts Phase 3's view to it:
   - it loads `room-data.json` once, the first time the resume opens, and shares that load with every later opening;
   - it shows "Loading the resume…" until the data arrives, then the view, unhidden;
   - it drops the view's own heading and dialog role, since the room's section frames it;
   - it wires the view's "Close" and "Download (PDF)" to the room;
   - it passes `room:item-selected` on as `item_selected`, for IDs the manifest lists;
   - it never calls `wireResumeOpeners`, so nothing opens the resume a second way or counts `room:resume-opened` again.

   In the room the view sits in the page's flow (`room.css` sets it `static`), not fixed over it, so the AI disclosure stays in view. `index.html`'s static "Read the resume" button, which only `wireResumeOpeners` served, is gone: the choice screen replaces that screen, and its PDF link stays. When `room-data.json` can't be read, the view's own message shows, "The resume couldn't be loaded. The PDF may still be available."
10. **Usage events** go to an `EventSink` (`src/room/events.ts`) in the shapes contracts/room-events.md §1 gives: `object_selected` and `item_selected` with their IDs, `resume_opened`, `resume_kept`, `pdf_downloaded`, `turn_sent`, `voice_pressed`, and `mode_2d`. The page's sink drops them until Phase 6's beacon (T060).
