## What changed and why

User Story 2's room and User Story 5's access to it, on the room's floor: everything is static, every object and surface answers with the spec's fixed messages, and nothing calls a model or sends a byte beyond the site's own files.

1. **The choice (T040; FR-001–FR-004).** `src/choice/`: the path ("Engineer", "Not an engineer") and the avatar, intent 000's four placeholders ("Woman", "Man", "Dinosaur", "Dog", each a flat shape with its name), as radio buttons in two fieldsets. "Enter" stays disabled until both are chosen, with the hint "Choose a path and an avatar to enter.". Beside them are "Download the resume (PDF)", which works without entering, and "How data flows". The AI disclosure is `index.html`'s header, on every screen. The visit (`visit.ts`, data-model.md §9) lives only in `startRoom`'s memory. The form is built by script and never submits, so no browser restores its choices on reload.
2. **The scene (T041; FR-005; R-11).** `src/room/scene.ts`, Three.js, all placeholder geometry: the desk with its laptop and screen, the snowboard hanging on the left, the bicycle on the right, the small window with artwork above it and its view of a fence and a tree, the twin's figure at the desk, and the visitor's chosen avatar as a flat cut-out in front. Each piece carries a label ending "(placeholder)", and the room's stage says "Placeholder shapes stand in for the room's real objects." Each object has an invisible hit box for raycasts. **Its own chunk:** the room imports `scene.ts` only after the WebGL 2 check. The entry script is 7.6 KB compressed and Three.js's chunk 145.8 KB, so a device without WebGL 2 never downloads it.
3. **The hand-off (T042; FR-007–FR-009; R-13).** `src/room/handoff.ts`: 4.5 s in 3D (the twin turns and the paper arcs into the visitor's hands), 1 s under reduced motion (a crossfade, nothing moves), and three still frames of 1.5 s in 2D. Its timeline has no way to end early. While it plays, the room's controls are `inert`, every handler also checks the visit's stage, and focus rests on the room's heading. Its line goes to the polite status region, which is in the page from the first screen. At its end, focus moves to "Read the resume now", beside "Keep it for later"; either one opens the room, and "Resume" stays in the toolbar from then on.
4. **Modes and quality (T043; FR-042, FR-048; R-04).** `src/room/quality.ts`: the controller steps quality 0 → 3, one step per 5-second window whose median frame rate is under the target (30 on a touch-primary device, 60 otherwise), and switches to 2D below 24 frames a second at quality 3. It never steps back up. Each step lowers the pixel ratio, then shadows, then textures and the idle sway. The WebGL 2 check uses `failIfMajorPerformanceCaveat`, so a software renderer starts in 2D. A lost context gets one restore within 3 s. `src/room/flat.ts` is the 2D mode: an inline SVG of the same placeholders, with a button over each object. "2D view" switches at any time, and every road to 2D sends one `mode_2d` and keeps the visit.
5. **The accessible layer and the layout (T044; FR-043–FR-047).** `src/room/a11y.ts` holds the "In the room" list, whose buttons call the same `selectObject` as a raycast hit and a touch, and the status region. `layout.ts` gives the order: toolbar, stage, list, conversation, resume. The canvas and the drawing are `aria-hidden`, and the drawing's buttons are `tabindex="-1"`, so the list is the objects' one accessible equivalent in both modes. `room.css`, `choice.css`, and `talk.css` give every target 44 by 44 CSS pixels and every focusable element a 2-pixel `#0b4f9c` outline. Portrait is the base layout, the stage's height in `dvh` leaving room for the toolbar, the list, the text box, and the talk button on a 320 by 568 screen; landscape puts the stage beside the list and the conversation. `framing.ts` moves the camera back until every piece is in view, from a 0.45 aspect to a wide desktop. Transitions are off under reduced motion.
6. **The conversation surfaces (T045; FR-023, FR-039, FR-040; R-14).** `src/talk/` and `content/messages.yaml`, the eight messages of data-model.md §7, each written in advance. An object answers `topic_not_ready` with its topic. A typed turn answers `cannot_talk.unavailable`, followed by "Read the resume" and "Download the resume (PDF)"; the visitor's text stays in the box, since it went nowhere. The talk button, on a pointer press, a held Space or Enter, or a screen reader's click, answers `voice_unavailable` once per press and moves focus to the text box, without touching the microphone. `cannotTalkFor` maps spec 000's degraded causes to the three classes for Phase 7.
7. **The manifest (T046).** `build/manifest.ts` builds `dist/room-manifest.json` (the three objects with `ready: false`, `free_talk.ready: false`, the resume's item IDs, the four avatars, `placeholders: true`, the eight event kinds) and the committed `config/room-events.yaml`, from one source: `src/`'s lists and the resume's item IDs. `npm run manifest -w apps/web` rewrites the YAML, and `test/manifest.test.ts` fails `make test` while it's stale, naming the first line that differs. The same plugin bundles the messages and the manifest into the page as `virtual:room-content`, so no answer needs a request.
8. **The resume's port.** `src/room/resume-port.ts`: the room owns the resume's panel (its heading "The resume", Escape, focus back to "Resume"), and a `ResumeView` renders inside it (`mount(host, {doc, path, close, pdfFollowed})`, `unmount()`). `stubResumeView()` stands in, with "The interactive resume isn't in this build yet.", "Download (PDF)", and "Close", until Phase 3's `src/resume/` arrives.
9. **Usage events** go to an `EventSink` (`src/room/events.ts`) in the shapes contracts/room-events.md §1 gives (`object_selected` with its ID, `resume_opened`, `resume_kept`, `pdf_downloaded`, `turn_sent`, `voice_pressed`, `mode_2d`). The page's sink drops them until Phase 6's beacon (T060).

**Shared files, kept small and additive:**
- `apps/web/package.json`: the script `"manifest": "node --import tsx build/manifest.ts"`, and the dev dependency `"yaml": "2.9.1"` (with the commas they need).
- `apps/web/src/main.ts`: the comment's third sentence now names `src/room/app.ts`; five lines import `virtual:room-content`, `startRoom`, and `stubResumeView`, and start the room.
- `apps/web/vite.config.ts`: one import, the plugin `roomContentModule(...)` after `roomDataFile(...)`, and `chunkSizeWarningLimit: 640` with its comment, since Three.js's chunk is meant to be large.
- `package-lock.json`: `"yaml": "2.9.1"` in `apps/web`'s dev dependencies, and nothing else; no package came from the registry.
- `apps/web/README.md`: one sentence, one bullet on the room's modules, and one command row.
- `apps/web/index.html`, `src/style.css`, `build/approvals.ts`, and the smoke test are untouched.

**The pin:** `yaml` 2.9.1 is the gateway's existing pin, which plan.md's Technical Context reuses ("yaml (already pinned for the gateway, 2.9.1, reused)"), not a new choice of the newest release. `apps/web` declares it itself because the image's `npm ci -w apps/web` installs only the workspace's own dependencies.

**Where the build went past the plan,** recorded in plan.md §4 ("What group 4 found") in 306c335: the messages are bundled rather than read from `room-data.json` (data-model.md §5's `messages` row now says so); the 3D chunk loads behind the choice; "below the target" allows 10% for frame-timing jitter; the 2D drawing's buttons are pointer targets only; the resume's port and the events' sink; no cloned-voice line until there's a voice; and six small modules beyond the plan's list.
