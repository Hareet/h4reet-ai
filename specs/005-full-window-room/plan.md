## Summary

- **One table decides the layout** (§2): `compose/hud.ts`, beside the supported stages, holds the HUD's placements for two layouts. **The column layout** has the AI disclosure as a strip at the top, the bar of controls at the foot, and one column on the right, at most 28rem, holding the list above the conversation. **The dock layout**, on a phone held upright, has the strip at the top and one dock at the foot: the list, the text box, then the bar. The runtime and the compositor read the same table; `room.css` takes its numbers through CSS properties the runtime sets.
- **The clear area is computed, not measured** (§3): the window less the docked boxes, from the table, so the room the visitor sees is framed exactly where the checks judged it. The supported stages become these clear areas. Today's `stageFor`, frozen in a test module, is the floor every screen of today's sweep is held to.
- **The canvas is the window** (§4): the room block is 100% of the window's width and its dynamic height. The buffer's ratio is the device's, capped by the quality level's ratio and by a per-level pixel cap. One `refit` path follows every change of the window, holding the quality controller while it does.
- **Framing into the clear area** (§5): one camera, its projection shifted with `setViewOffset`, so the room is centred in the clear area and the scene continues behind the panels. The picker, the walking view's taps, and its follow camera take the same shift.
- **The HUD** (§6): dark glass at the opacity floor the contrast test computes (0.60 to start), a blur only at quality levels 0 and 1, and game-style tiles in CSS, an icon drawn in the repository over each control's words. It also covers the collapse toggles, the log's sheet and its larger view, the resume over the room, the safe areas, the keyboard's inset, and the stacked layout, which is today's grid kept as the fallback.
- **One place for each press** (§7): the panels are siblings over the canvas, so they take their own events, and `objects.ts`'s pointer capture keeps a press that started on the scene the scene's. Tests prove what already holds, and the plan adds the few guards that don't.
- **Full screen** (§8): `fullscreen.ts`. The page's root goes full screen, the control is offered only where the browser reports support, and Alt+Enter is the shortcut. One guard, `browserOwnsEscape`, makes every Escape handler leave Escape to the browser while in full screen.
- **The checks** (§10): RG6's reservation covers any HUD control over the clear area, RG5's aspects cover the new range, the sweep extends to 5120 × 1440, and the lookdev page gains a window view.
- **The event, the panel, and the row** (§12): `fullscreen_entered`, in the same steps spec 008's `walk_started` took, with row V4's trigger gaining it before spec 009's clause.

**What the Author does** (§15): approves; gates and merges the branch; then looks at the live room (SC-013) and runs the lookdev page on the reference phone and a large monitor (SC-008). No patch of his is required.

### 2.2 The functions

- `layoutFor(screen)`: `"column"` when `screen.w > screen.h` and `screen.w ≥ 32rem`, else `"dock"`.
- `hudBoxes(screen, state, metrics)`: each docked panel's box in CSS pixels, from the table, the window, the panels' state (`{ list: "open" | "collapsed" }` in the dock layout; nothing in the column layout), and the metrics (`rem`, and the safe-area insets; both default to 16 and 0 for the compositor).
- `clearArea(screen, state, metrics)`: the window less those boxes, as `{ x, y, w, h }`.
- `fitsHud(screen, state, metrics)`: the clear area at least the `minimum`; false selects the stacked layout.
- `reservedBoxes(clear)`: the HUD controls that lie over the clear area, in its pixels: today the walking control's box alone, `walkControlBox(clear)`. If D10's fallback puts the full-screen control beside it, that box joins (FR-055).

### 2.3 How `room.css` follows the table

### 4.3 What triggers a re-fit (`hud/viewport.ts`; PD-14, PD-15)

## 5. Framing into the clear area (PD-12, PD-13)

### 5.1 The camera

`frameCamera(camera, clear, canvas, boxes)` sets the camera from `framing(clear.w / clear.h, boxes)`, as now. It then calls `camera.setViewOffset(clear.w, clear.h, −clear.x, −clear.y, canvas.w, canvas.h)` and `updateProjectionMatrix()`. Three's projection widens the clear area's frustum by the canvas's extra pixels on each side, at the same scale, so the room keeps its size and place in the clear area, and the scene, the backdrop included, continues under the panels. A canvas equal to the clear area (the stacked layout; the 2D room's fallback) clears the offset. T001 reads `PerspectiveCamera.updateProjectionMatrix` in the pinned three to confirm a negative offset widens as stated.

`viewOffsetFor(clear, canvas)` in `fit.ts` returns the five numbers, and `test/room/fit.test.ts` checks them. It also projects each framed box's corners through the shifted projection and checks that each lands inside the clear area, with the margin (SC-001).

### 5.2 The picker

`selectablePicker`'s areas stay in the clear area's pixels, at the camera `framing` gives for the clear area, so they are RG6's numbers. `pick(ndc)` now takes the canvas's normalized point. `canvasToClear(ndc, canvas, clear)` turns it into the clear area's pixels, and a point outside the clear area picks nothing. `bindPointerSelection` is unchanged: it still reports the canvas's point, from the canvas's rectangle.

### 5.3 The walking view

`createWalking`'s `production(stage)` takes the clear area, so the farthest distance follows its aspect (spec 008 FR-023). `scene.ts`'s `applyCamera` sets the follow camera's aspect to the clear area's and applies the same view offset, so the figure stays centred in the clear area (FR-018). A missed click's ray (`ray(camera, aspect, [x, y])`) takes the tap moved into the clear area's normalized coordinates first, so the figure walks to the floor point under the pointer.

### 5.4 The 2D room

`layout.ts` puts the 2D stage's element (`.flat`), the `flat-failed` line, and the loading line in a clear box. The clear box is an absolutely placed element at `--clear-*`, with `container-type: size`, so `.flat`'s `min(100cqw, 160cqh)` sizes the drawing in the clear area as today. `flat.ts` needs no change: its picker measures its own element, and its observer follows the clear box. ## 8. Full screen (`fullscreen.ts`; PD-19 to PD-21)

### 8.1 The module

`createFullscreen(doc)` returns:
- `supported`: `doc.fullscreenEnabled === true`, or the prefixed `webkitFullscreenEnabled`, with a request function present;
- `active`, read from `fullscreenElement` or `webkitFullscreenElement`;
- `toggle()`: `documentElement.requestFullscreen({ navigationUI: "hide" })`, or `exitFullscreen()`. Its promise's rejection, and a `fullscreenerror`, call `onRefused`;
- `onChange(listener)`: listens to `fullscreenchange`, or WebKit's prefixed event.

It never calls `navigator.keyboard.lock`, `screen.orientation.lock`, or `requestPointerLock`, which the test asserts against spies (FR-041).

### 8.2 The control

The control is built only when `supported` (Q4, provisional), as a tile after "Pause the room". Its words are `fullscreen.enter` or `fullscreen.leave`, set on every change, by any means (FR-040). It carries `aria-keyshortcuts="Alt+Enter"`, and its description names the shortcut through a visually hidden span. It is inert with the room's other controls during the hand-off and its offer.

### 8.3 The shortcut (PD-20)

`app.ts` adds the capture-phase `keydown` listener when the room is built. It acts only when the room is open (`open()`, as the controls' handlers check), `supported`, and the key is Alt+Enter with no other modifier. It calls `preventDefault()` and `stopPropagation()`, then `toggle()`.

### 8.4 Escape (PD-21; D3)

`browserOwnsEscape(doc)` is true while `active`. Before acting on Escape, the walking input (`walk/input.ts`'s `escape` intent), the talk form's Escape, the resume panel's Escape (`app.ts`), and the larger view's and the sheets' Escape each check it. Spec 008's announcements switch to `walk.entered_fullscreen`, and the walking view's description gains `walk.help_fullscreen`, while `active`.

### 8.5 A refusal (FR-005)

`onRefused` says `fullscreen.refused` once through the room's polite status region, and nothing else changes; focus stays where it was.

### 8.6 The event (FR-060)

On the first change to `active` in a visit, `app.ts` emits `{ kind: "fullscreen_entered" }`, as `enterWalking` emits `walk_started`.
