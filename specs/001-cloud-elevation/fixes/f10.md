# Fix F10: the tunnel end lets go of a dead laptop session's forwards

Spec 001, a bug-fix found on the deployed site on 2026-11-18. Branch `fix/001-live-network`, from main `f1abe245`, with F9. The brief came from the Author's session; spec 001's `plan.md` indexes this file under "Bug-fixes, 2026-11-18".

**Superseded in part by fix F11** ([f11.md](f11.md), 2026-11-22): this fix's second part, the net that ended the older sessions on sshd's log line, is removed, and so is the tunnel end's count of accepted logins. Under `-e` sshd leaves a stranger's newlines as sent, so a stranger on 2222 could write the line the net acted on. The first part, `tcp_retries2` 8, stays.

## The evidence, as the brief gives it

About 03:10 to 03:20Z on 2026-11-18, after the laptop's Windows froze and was hard-restarted, the laptop's tunnel client (`deploy/tunnel/entrypoint.sh`, client mode; `ssh_config`'s `ExitOnForwardFailure yes`) reconnected every 30 s. The tunnel end's sshd accepted the key each time, then logged `bind [<address>]<address> Address already in use` and `channel_setup_fwd_listener_tcpip: cannot listen to port: 2201` (and 2202, 2203), so each connection ended within a second. The old session held the three ports for 15 minutes and more, despite `ClientAliveInterval 10` and `ClientAliveCountMax 3`, until `h4reet-prod-tunnel-end-1` was restarted by hand.

## The cause

`deploy/tunnel/sshd_config`, read whole: nothing overrides the client-alive settings (no `Match`, no `Include`; the tests already hold that), `TCPKeepAlive` is the default, yes (its probes start after two idle hours and only on an idle connection, so they play no part), `GatewayPorts clientspecified` and the `PermitListen` lines only say where the forwards may bind, and `StreamLocalBindUnlink` concerns Unix sockets, which `AllowStreamLocalForwarding no` refuses. With `MaxSessions 0` there's no shell or command, so no lingering per-connection process beyond sshd's own: the forwards are held by the session's own sshd process, and end with it.

So the question is why that process outlived client-alive's 30 to 40 s. **A hypothesis, not verified** (the review of bd7e0765, nit 3): there is no OpenSSH 9.6 source on this machine and no network to fetch it, only the client package of the same version (`openssh-client 1:9.6p1-3ubuntu13.20`). From memory of OpenSSH's server loop (`serverloop.c`, `wait_until_can_do_something`): the probe is sent when the loop's `ppoll` times out, and the timeout is set again to `ClientAliveInterval` on every pass, so any other descriptor's activity can wake it first. On the tunnel end the relay checks the laptop through `tunnel-end:2201` every second (`deploy/compose.yaml`'s `RELAY_LAPTOP_URL`; `services/relay/src/hosts.ts`, `CHECK_INTERVAL_MS = 1_000`), so the dead session's listener accepted a connection, and tried to open a channel to the laptop, every second: if the probe needs a quiet `ppoll`, it never ran. **The caveat:** recent versions of that loop may also probe on other wakeups once the last word from the client is older than the interval; in a version that does, client-alive would have ended the session in about 40 s, and this reading doesn't explain the night. What does fit, either way, is the 15 minutes.

What ended it instead was TCP: each channel open was data sent to the dead laptop, unacknowledged, retransmitted with backoff, until the kernel gave up after `net.ipv4.tcp_retries2`'s 15 tries, about 924.6 s from the first: the "15 minutes and more" of the night. The restart came first.

Neither part of the fix depends on the hypothesis: the first bounds the session's life by the kernel's timer whatever sshd does, and the second ends it on the evidence itself, a new session refused its forwards.

Two parts, then: the cause bounded where this repository can reach it, and a safety net for whatever is left.

## The fix

1. **The tunnel end's container gives up on a silent peer in under two minutes** (`deploy/compose.yaml`, `tunnel-end`'s `sysctls: net.ipv4.tcp_retries2: "8"`). The setting is per network namespace, so it touches the container's sockets alone (sshd's, and its forwarded connections to the relay and the collector inside the stack's bridges), never the host's. Eight retries from the kernel's 200 ms minimum are (2⁹ − 1) × 0.2 s ≈ 102 s. The compose policy (`tools/gates/compose_policy.py`) forbids no `sysctls` key, and Docker allows `net.*` in a container's own namespace.
2. **A new session that can't listen ends the older ones** (`deploy/tunnel/entrypoint.sh`, server mode). The loop that reads sshd's log, after `scrub`, now calls `end_older_sessions` on a line that `holds_forward` recognizes: the whole line `channel_setup_fwd_listener_tcpip: cannot listen to port: 2201` (or `2202`, `2203`, PermitListen's three), with sshd's optional trailing `\r`, and nothing else. Under `-e` an error-level line has no `error: ` prefix (OpenSSH's `log.c` names the level only for syslog), which is how the night's journal shows it, and how the 9.6 client prints the same function's line for a local forward. **Anchored, after the review of bd7e0765 (I1):** 2222 is open to anywhere, and sshd logs a stranger's user name and identification as sent, so the text inside another line (`Invalid user cannot listen to port: 2201 from …`, `Connection closed by invalid user … [preauth]`, `kex_exchange_identification: client sent invalid protocol identifier "…"`) must never end the laptop's tunnel. ~~sshd escapes control characters in what it logs, so a stranger can't start a line of their own.~~ **Wrong** ([f11.md](f11.md)): sshd escapes them in syslog's lines only; under `-e` it leaves newline and carriage return as sent, so a stranger's KEXINIT offer or disconnect description can start a line of their own, and fix F11 removed this net. It finds the listening sshd (the oldest `sshd` whose parent is the entrypoint), and ends with SIGTERM every process the daemon started more than `GRACE` seconds before, with their own processes. `GRACE` is sshd's `LoginGraceTime`, 10 s, so a session still setting up (the one that logged the conflict among them) is never touched, and any older one has already asked for, and holds, its forwards. Each process is signalled once (reset at each accepted login), and the loop says which, with process numbers only. It reads `/proc/<pid>/stat` (start time, parent), which any user may read, and signals processes of its own user, so it needs no capability. Every session is the tunnel user's: `AllowUsers tunnel`.

Why ending the older session is right: the laptop's client holds one connection at a time (its loop starts a new `ssh` only when the last one has ended), so a new connection asking for the three forwards means the laptop has let go of the old one. Its next try comes 1 to 30 s later (`BACKOFF=(1 2 5 10 30)`) and takes them.

The admin-only stack's client (`ssh_config_admin`) asks for no remote forward, so it never logs the conflict; if it is connected when the laptop's conflict comes, it is ended too, and reconnects by its own loop.

### The tests (`deploy/tunnel/test_tunnel.py`)

- The tunnel end's compose service sets `net.ipv4.tcp_retries2` to 8, and the kernel's bound for 8 is between 60 and 120 s (for 15, over 900).
- `GRACE` is `sshd_config`'s `LoginGraceTime`.
- `holds_forward`, on sshd's lines through `scrub`: the three ports' conflict yes, with or without sshd's trailing `\r`; 5432's and 22010's, the `bind` line, an accepted login, a stranger's `Invalid user`, `Connection closed by invalid user … [preauth]`, and `kex_exchange_identification` lines carrying the text, and the conflict with anything before or after it, no.
- `stale_sessions`, on a made-up `/proc`: the daemon's children started before the limit and every process under them; not the daemon, not a new session or its child, not another parent's `sshd`; a command holding `) ` read right. `daemon_pid` picks the oldest `sshd` under the entrypoint.
- Server mode under bash with a stand-in `sshd` that starts an old session, then a new one, then prints sshd's accepted and `bind` lines and either two conflict lines or a stranger's three: with the conflict, the old session ends, the new one is alive, and the entrypoint says so once, naming the old pid; with the stranger's text, both live and nothing is said. The address in the accepted line is scrubbed.
- On the agents' engine (skips here): the fixture's client paused, a second client of the same key started at once; within 25 s the second one's 2201 reaches the edge stand-in, and the server's log holds the conflict and the safety net's line; then the first client is unpaused and its forwards come back.

## The Author's morning steps

With F9's release (one release carries both: the tunnel end's image is rebuilt from `deploy/tunnel/`). The laptop's `tunnel` service builds the same image locally; its client mode doesn't change, so it needs no rebuild for this fix.

1. **On the tunnel end,** after the release (his Systems Manager session, as root):

   ```bash
   T=h4reet-prod-tunnel-end-1
   F=/proc/sys/net/ipv4/tcp_retries2
   docker exec "$T" cat "$F"
   cat "$F"
   ```

   `8` inside the container, and the host's own value (15 by default) unchanged.

2. **The reconnect test,** on the laptop, its prod stack up, the tunnel connected. First the plain kill:

   ```bash
   C=h4reet-prod-tunnel-1
   docker kill "$C"
   docker start "$C"
   ```

   Within about 30 s, `docker logs --since 2m "$C"` shows no "trying again" loop, and on the tunnel end `docker logs --since 2m "$T"` shows one `Accepted publickey for tunnel` and no `cannot listen`. A kill closes the connection cleanly (the laptop's kernel answers for the dead process), so this checks the reconnect, not the night's case.

3. **The night's case, a peer that goes silent:**

   ```bash
   N=h4reet-prod_egress
   docker network disconnect "$N" "$C"
   sleep 45
   docker network connect "$N" "$C"
   ```

   The laptop's client gives up on its silent connection after 30 s (`ServerAliveInterval 10`, `ServerAliveCountMax 3`) and retries; once the network is back, either the tunnel end has already let go (about 100 s after its first unanswered packet), or its log shows one `cannot listen to port: 2201` followed by `tunnel-end: a new session couldn't listen on its forwards; ended the older session holding them`, and the next connection holds. Either way the tunnel is back within about two minutes of the reconnect, with no restart. `/api/status` returns to serving the laptop when the relay counts it healthy again.

## Done, and not done

**Done:** the dead session's life bounded to about 100 s while data is outstanding, in the tunnel end's own namespace; the server ends older sessions the moment a new one can't listen on 2201 to 2203, so the laptop's next try gets its forwards without a human; tests for both, pure where they can be.

**Not run here:** the container check (no agents' engine), and anything on the real tunnel end. Why client-alive didn't end the session is a hypothesis ("The cause"), not re-read from the 9.6 source; the 15 minutes matching `tcp_retries2`'s 924.6 s is what the fix's first part rests on. The raw line's form (no `error: ` prefix) rests on the night's journal and OpenSSH's stderr logging; if a real line ever carried the prefix, the safety net would stay silent and the first part would still end the session in about 100 s. His steps 2 and 3 are the proof.

## Backlog

- **F10-1:** two live clients with the same key (the laptop's prod and a second stack's `tunnel`, say) would now take the forwards from each other at each reconnect, instead of the second one failing. Each takeover is logged; the key is meant for one client.
- **F10-2:** read `serverloop.c` at 9.6p1 (or watch `Timeout, client not responding` in the tunnel end's log during step 3) to settle the hypothesis; if client-alive does fire on other wakeups, the night's 15 minutes need another explanation, and `tcp_retries2` and the safety net still bound it.
- **F10-3:** the laptop's own client (its `ServerAliveInterval` is checked by deadline on the client side) needs nothing; if the laptop's tunnel ever keeps a dead connection, `tcp_retries2` on the `tunnel` service is the same one-line change.
