# Fix F11: the tunnel end decides nothing from sshd's log

Spec 001, a security finding on fix F10's code, which went live on 2026-11-22. Branch `fix/001-f11-log-trigger`, from main `37e4a623`. The brief came from the Author's session, with a validator's confirmation, and asks for the fix before the repository goes public; spec 001's `plan.md` indexes this file under "Bug-fixes, 2026-11-22".

## The finding, as the brief gives it

> F10's tunnel-end safety net makes decisions from sshd's log text, which a stranger can forge without logging in.

The validator's evidence, in the brief's words:

> - With -e, OpenSSH 9.6p1 logs to stderr with VIS_SAFE|VIS_OCTAL, which leaves \n, \r, tab, BS and BEL unescaped (only syslog adds VIS_NL). Confirmed on this machine's openssh 9.6p1 build: ssh-keygen's fatal() printed \n raw. So an unauthenticated client on port 2222 (open to 0.0.0.0/0) can start a line of its own: a failed KEXINIT logs "Unable to negotiate ... Their offer: <peer's list>", and SSH_MSG_DISCONNECT logs "Received disconnect from ...: %.400s". The injected `channel_setup_fwd_listener_tcpip: cannot listen to port: 2201` passes scrub and matches. Reproduced in a harness […]: the laptop's session got SIGTERM, the stranger survived. Repeating every few seconds keeps the laptop's tunnel up ~10 s in 41 and cuts in-flight answers.
> - The `*"Accepted publickey for tunnel"*` case is a substring match, forgeable even without a newline (identification string, user name): it inflates the accepted counter behind h4.tunnel.reconnects and resets SIGNALLED.
> - The comment at entrypoint.sh ~103-104 and fixes/f10.md ~24 claim sshd escapes control characters; that's true only for syslog.

The validator's harness, run here against main's entrypoint before the fix, printed the same: with each of its two newline records, `channel_setup_fwd_listener_tcpip: cannot listen to port: 2201` reached `holds_forward` as a line of its own, the laptop's stand-in session was ended, and the stranger's lived; with the forged accepted lines, the reported reconnections went from 0 to 2.

## The cause

F10 read sshd's log in two places, both in server mode's loop over sshd's output after `scrub`:

- **The net.** `holds_forward` took the whole line `channel_setup_fwd_listener_tcpip: cannot listen to port: 220[123]` as sshd's own, and `end_older_sessions` then sent SIGTERM to every session the listening sshd had started more than `GRACE` (10 s) before. F10's review (I1) anchored the match to the whole line, on the belief that sshd escapes control characters in what it logs. It does in syslog's lines, not on stderr: under `-e`, OpenSSH's `log.c` escapes a record with `VIS_SAFE|VIS_OCTAL`, which leaves newline and carriage return as they came, and writes it with its `\r\n` in one write. A newline inside text a stranger chose starts a line of its own for the loop's `read`.
- **The count.** The loop counted `Accepted publickey for tunnel` anywhere in a line, so a stranger's identification line or user name moved `h4.tunnel.reconnects`, newline or not, and cleared the net's memory of whom it had signalled (`SIGNALLED`).

What a stranger with no key chooses in sshd's log: the name-list after `Their offer: ` when their KEXINIT matches nothing of the server's (`packet.c`'s fatal error, `Unable to negotiate with %s: %s. Their offer: %s`, which the monitor logs with ` [preauth]`); the description of an `SSH_MSG_DISCONNECT` (`Received disconnect from %s port %d:%u: %.400s`); a user name (`Invalid user %.100s from …`); and an identification line (`kex_exchange_identification: client sent invalid protocol identifier "%.256s"`, which can't hold a newline, but a substring was enough for the count). The three formats from `packet.c` and `kex.c`, code the client shares with sshd, are read from the installed 9.6p1 client's binary (`openssh-client 1:9.6p1-3ubuntu13.20`), with `no matching key exchange method found`; `Invalid user`, sshd's alone, and which process logs what with ` [preauth]` are from memory of OpenSSH's source (there's no 9.6 source here, and no sshd may run on this machine). So any line of sshd's log may be a stranger's, and no matcher on it can tell. The fix isn't a better matcher: nothing on the tunnel end decides from the log.

## The fix

1. **Server mode only scrubs and prints sshd's log** (`deploy/tunnel/entrypoint.sh`): `/usr/sbin/sshd -D -e -f /etc/ssh/h4/sshd_config 2>&1 | scrub`, with nothing after it. F10's `holds_forward`, `stat_of`, `daemon_pid`, `stale_sessions`, `end_older_sessions`, `PROC`, `GRACE`, and `SIGNALLED` are gone, and so is the accepted file. Kept: F10's `net.ipv4.tcp_retries2: "8"` on the tunnel end's compose service, and sshd's `ClientAliveInterval 10` and `ClientAliveCountMax 3`.
2. **No net in its place** (the choice, below).
3. **The tunnel end's count comes from the kernel's table.** At each 15-second report, `forward_socket` reads the inode of the socket listening on 2201 from `/proc/net/tcp`. A session that takes the forwards listens on sockets of its own, so an inode other than the last report's, after the first, is a reconnection. Only an authenticated session may listen on 2201 (`PermitListen`), and nothing a stranger sends adds a listener. `listening`, behind `h4.tunnel.up`, already read the same table; both now read it through `TCP_TABLE`.
4. **The false claim corrected:** the entrypoint's comment went with `holds_forward`; f10.md now says, where it made the claim, that it's wrong, and points here.

### A faster net than the kernel's, weighed

What frees a dead session's forwards without F10's net:

- **client-alive**, in 30 to 40 s, when its probe runs. f10.md's hypothesis, still open (F10-2), is that the relay's check through 2201 every second keeps it from running.
- **the kernel**, when the peer is silent. With `tcp_retries2` 8 the kernel's model gives up after 102.2 s from a 200 ms timeout (f10.md). It checks at each retransmission, whose interval doubles from the connection's own timeout (its round trip plus at least 200 ms), so the session ends between 102 and 204 s after the first unanswered packet: about two minutes for a home link's round trip, at most three and a half. Then the laptop's client takes the forwards at its next try, at most 30 s later. This wasn't measured here: the agents' engine refuses a container's sysctls (its authorization plugin's rule A19), so the bound is the kernel's arithmetic, and the Author's step 1 below reads the value live.

The night of 2026-11-18 was a silent peer: the laptop's Windows froze and was hard-restarted, and the 15 minutes match the kernel's default `tcp_retries2` (f10.md). At 8, the old session ends about two minutes after the freeze, which is about as long as a hard-restarted laptop takes to bring Windows, Docker, its stack, and the tunnel client back. A shorter silence (a network blip, a changed address) costs the laptop's host that bound and a backoff, during which the relay serves EC2 or rests, as whenever the laptop is unhealthy. Nothing in f10.md's evidence calls for a faster net.

A net that decides from state alone was weighed, as the validator described it, and isn't built:

- **Which process to end.** Only `/proc/<pid>/fd` names the process that holds the forwards' sockets, and sshd may make its processes undumpable, which hides that folder from their own user (F9 and F10's note asked this; unverified here, with no sshd to run).
- **"A newer authenticated tunnel session exists"** can't be read from state. The laptop's refused session ends within a second (`ExitOnForwardFailure`), and in the process table it looks like a stranger's unauthenticated one. And the admin-only stack's client is a newer authenticated session that asks for no remote forward: the condition would end a healthy laptop session whenever it connects.
- **"Its own 2222 connection is retransmitting"** (the row's retransmission count) is a second, faster `tcp_retries2` for one socket, with a threshold to pick against home-network outages (F10 chose 8 over 5 for that reason), a polling loop, and tests on made-up `/proc` tables and fd links.
- **What it buys:** a minute or two, in a case that is rare, for a mechanism that ends the laptop's session on its own judgment, which is what F10's net got wrong.

The brief says "The Author prefers right-sized fixes": the kernel's bound is that. A peer that still acknowledges but never reads, as F10's container check made one by pausing its client, is ended only by client-alive, if its probe runs. That isn't a production case: the laptop's client starts a new connection only after its ssh has exited, and the laptop's kernel then closes that socket, or, if the laptop is gone, leaves it silent. F10's container check, which paused one client and started a second with the same key, tested the removed net, and goes with it.

### The count, weighed

Dropping the tunnel end's count was weighed too. The laptop's own count covers the laptop's connections exactly, but it reaches only the laptop's collector, while the tunnel end's own records go to both hosts (`deploy/otel/collector-tunnel-end.yaml`): on EC2's dashboard, which the laptop's collector doesn't feed, the tunnel end's series is the tunnel's only one. So it stays, from state. What it counts has changed, and the panel says so: two reconnections between reports count once, and a session refused the forwards (the night's) isn't counted on the tunnel end, while the laptop's series counts each. The "Tunnel reconnects" panel's description (`deploy/grafana/dashboards/h4-runtime.json`) and contracts/tunnel.md §7 say what each end counts; the panel's query is unchanged.

### The tests (`deploy/tunnel/test_tunnel.py`)

- **No line of sshd's log ends a session or moves the count:** server mode under bash, with a stand-in sshd that starts an old session (the laptop's, past F10's grace) and a new one, prints the new one's accepted login, then writes one record whole, in one write, as sshd `-e` does. Eight records: a KEXINIT's offer and a disconnect's description, each carrying the conflict line on a line of its own, with `\n` and with `\r\n`; `Accepted publickey for tunnel` in an identification line, in a user name (`Invalid user …` and `Connection closed by invalid user … [preauth]`), and on lines of its own; and sshd's own conflict line. For each: both sessions live, the entrypoint says nothing of its own but, at most, `tunnel-end: sshd stopped`, every report (every 0.2 s here, against a table where one session holds the forwards) says 0 reconnections, and the log reaches the output scrubbed.
- **The tunnel end's reconnections are new sessions holding the forwards:** `report` under bash, its wait stubbed to put the next of six `/proc/net/tcp` tables in place: the first holder (0), the same holder with the relay's connection through 2201 listed first (0), a gap (down, 0), a new holder (1), the same with a listener on 22010 listed first (1), another holder (2).
- **The server only scrubs and prints sshd's log:** the pipeline ends at `scrub`, and no line of the entrypoint's code signals a process.
- Gone with the net: F10's tests of `GRACE`, `holds_forward`, `stale_sessions` and `daemon_pid`, the server-mode test that expected the old session ended, and the container check that paused a client. Kept: F10's `tcp_retries2` test.
- **No container check with the real sshd.** The tunnel's image can't be built on the agents' engine (its egress proxy doesn't reach Ubuntu's archive) and isn't seeded there (`h4reet-tunnel-seed:local` is absent), so the tunnel's eight container checks skip, and a raw-socket stranger against the real sshd isn't added. The stand-in writes the records as `log.c` writes them; the validator's probe of the installed 9.6p1 `log.c` showed the raw newline. Backlog F11-1.

## The Author's steps

One release carries it: the tunnel end's image is rebuilt from `deploy/tunnel/`, and the laptop's `tunnel` service builds the same folder. Client mode is unchanged.

1. **On the tunnel end,** in his Systems Manager session, as root, after the release:

   ```bash
   T=h4reet-prod-tunnel-end-1
   B=/usr/local/bin/h4-tunnel
   docker exec "$T" grep -c holds_forward "$B"
   F=/proc/sys/net/ipv4/tcp_retries2
   docker exec "$T" cat "$F"
   ```

   `0` (the new entrypoint) and `8`. The first `grep` exits 1 when it counts nothing; that is the expected answer.

2. **The reconnect,** on the laptop, its prod stack up (f10.md's step 2): `docker kill h4reet-prod-tunnel-1`, then `docker start` it. The tunnel is back within about 30 s, and on the dashboard, "Tunnel reconnects" rises by one on the `tunnel-end` series within a report or two.
3. **A silent peer** (f10.md's step 3, its expectation changed): the laptop's tunnel container off its egress network for 45 s, then back. The tunnel end's log may show `cannot listen to port: 2201` lines while the old session holds the forwards, and nothing from the entrypoint about ending sessions; the tunnel is back within about four minutes of the cut, with no restart.

## Done, and not done

**Done:** nothing on the tunnel end reads sshd's log for a decision: no session is ended on its text, and the tunnel end's reconnections come from the kernel's table of listening sockets, which only an authenticated session changes. F10's `tcp_retries2` 8 and sshd's client-alive stay. The panel and the contract say what each end counts. Tests for both, under bash, with records written as sshd writes them.

**Not run here:** anything with the real sshd (no image on the agents' engine; no sshd may run on this machine), the kernel's bound in a container (the engine refuses sysctls), and anything on the tunnel end. The record formats are the installed 9.6p1 client's strings where its code is sshd's (above), and otherwise memory of OpenSSH's source; the raw newline is the validator's probe of the installed 9.6p1 `log.c`.
