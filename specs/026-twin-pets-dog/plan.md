## 1. Summary

Two halves, one selection.

1. **The petting (code, on the line).** A pure *pet director* takes a selection of the `dog` selectable and, where both figures show their models at home, the dog's home within `reach_m` of the twin's, and motion is allowed at quality level 0, turns the twin toward the dog through `faceTwin`, the one input this spec adds to spec 025's director, and runs the twin's `petting` state: the studio's `petting` clip where his approval bundles one, else a *reach layer* over the idle, which bends the arm on the dog's side to the dog's head by a two-bone reach, leans the spine, strokes, and withdraws. Then the twin turns back. The dog stays at its home throughout (Q159). A run from the dinosaur ends it. Everything tunable is scene data in `figures.pet`.
2. **The dog-intro (data and corpus, in one human patch).** The code reads an optional `figure` on a selectable, so the room's dog figure, at home, is a second pick target for the selectable `dog` (spec 025's FR-018, amended by M5), front-only and measured by RG6 at its home (Q160); the selectable's scene line, the corpus proposal's lines and slot, and everything they regenerate travel together in one patch for his hand (spec Q7). Its "Do not claim" line is in every topic's hash, so the patch closes all three ready topics; he applies it back to back with spec 022's and evaluates once (Q141).

No dependency is added; nothing leaves the device for the petting until his approval bundles the studio's clip, a twin file row V1 already covers (spec FR-025); the conversation is a twin turn like any piece's.

- **R-03 The reach.** For the arm on the dog's side: shoulder S (the `Arm` bone's world position), upper length a and fore length b (+ the hand's to the middle finger's base, from the rig at rest), target T on the dog's head (the adapter's head point, §4, lowered 0.03 m and moved 0.05 m toward the twin). First the spine lean: the smallest lean about the hips' side axis, shared over `Spine`, `Spine1`, `Spine2` in equal parts, up to `lean_max_deg`, that brings |T − S′| ≤ 0.98 (a + b). `lean_max_deg`'s default is measured (Q162; spec FR-007): T003 reads the twin's and the man's shoulder heights, a, b, and the hips' height from their rigs at rest, computes for each the lean that brings the head point of the dog at its default home (the twin turned by `faceTwin`) within 0.98 (a + b), and sets the default to the larger plus 5°, capped at 75°, recording the measured numbers in §3. If even 75° leaves either figure short, T003 stops RED with the numbers in its notes and a question on the page (Q162's other answers: a crouch through the hips and knees, or SC-002 relaxed to the nearest point); SC-002 isn't relaxed by a worker; then the two-bone solve in the plane of S′, T, and a pole 0.3 m out and down from the elbow (law of cosines for the elbow, an aim for the shoulder), the elbow clamped to `elbow_deg`; the hand's own rotation keeps the clip's, turned palm-down by the forearm's twist only. A target beyond reach: the arm straight toward it. The stroke moves T along the dog's heading by `stroke_m · sin(2π · n · u)` for the stroke's share u; reach-in and withdraw blend the layer's weight 0 → 1 → 0 by a smoothstep over 0.5 s. Pure functions over numbers (`solveLean`, `solveTwoBone`, `strokeTarget`, `weightAt`) are tested alone; chosen over three's `CCDIKSolver`, which iterates, allocates, and needs a skinned mesh's IK data the rig doesn't carry.
