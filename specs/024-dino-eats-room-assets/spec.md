Summary: a visitor who has chosen the Dinosaur can walk up to the desk, the busy sign, the laptop, or the chalkboard and eat a chunk of it. At the first bite, the piece's own model is divided: the parts that hold the room together (each live face's mesh, what holds that face up, what carries a mounted piece, and what holds a glow) are set aside and stay where they are, each as visible as the mesh it came from; the rest is split, by shape, into a handful of chunks. The dinosaur's bite takes one chunk, which flies to its mouth and is gone; the rest of the piece stays where it was. The room is never left broken: pieces grow back by themselves, one chunk at a time, and the set-aside part is never eaten, so what the visitor can select and read, and the laptop's glows, are always there, each on what holds it. Every degraded path (2D room, reduced motion, a failed or missing model, a dinosaur still without its model) removes the eating and nothing else. Cloud cost: $0. Nothing leaves the device for the eating: no request and no usage event (page Q117, over Q108; a named deviation, Assumptions A5).

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Eat a Chunk of a Piece (Priority: P1)

As a visitor who chose the Dinosaur, I want to walk up to the desk and bite a piece out of it.

**Why this priority**: it is the Author's item.

**Independent Test**: `vitest run` on the chunker and the eating driver, with a stub piece, a stub dinosaur, and a stub stage: split a stub mesh, take one chunk, and read the piece's meshes: one fewer chunk, the other chunks' vertices unchanged. By hand, on a dev-cpu stack with the dinosaur's model: walk to the desk, press B.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the figure is the dinosaur and has walked up to the desk, **When** the visitor presses B (or the "Eat" tile), **Then** one of the desk's chunks flies to the dinosaur's mouth over at most 600 ms and is gone, the dinosaur holds its idle (FR-013), and the rest of the desk is where it was.
2. **Given** the figure is the woman, the man, or the dog, **When** the visitor presses B at the desk, **Then** nothing happens and no "Eat" tile shows.
3. **Given** the figure has reached the monitor (not edible), **When** the visitor presses B, **Then** nothing happens.
4. **Given** every chunk of the desk has been eaten, so only its set-aside part stands, **When** the visitor presses B, **Then** nothing is eaten and the "Eat" tile hides.
5. **Given** any visit, **When** the dinosaur eats, **Then** no request and no usage event leaves the device for it (FR-014).

### User Story 2 - The Room Is Never Left Broken (Priority: P1)

As the Author and as any visitor, I want the room to stay whole where it matters, so the twin, the labels, the laptop's glows, and the picking always work.

**Why this priority**: constitution II (production-grade), and the room carries the tier sentence and the sign words.

**Independent Test**: `vitest run` on the regrowth and protection tests: for each of the four pieces, eat every chunk, then select the piece, read its label, and read the laptop's glows and what holds them; wait the regrowth timer and read the chunks back in order.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** every chunk of each of the four pieces is eaten, **When** the visitor selects the piece from the scene or the "In the room" list, **Then** it selects as before, with its label and topic, and its pick area is unchanged.
2. **Given** the laptop's placement binds no face (spec 004's FR-031: "THE LAPTOP'S SCREEN, the second monitor, SHALL be a live face that stays a plain glow unless approved room text is bound to it"), so its screen shows its glow card and no quad, **When** its chunks are eaten, **Then** the screen's glow and the charge light each stay on what holds them: the screen's glow on the `screen` and the lid behind it, the charge light on its `charge-light` mesh and the base around it, which still glows while the plug is in (spec 004's switch `laptop-power`). The busy sign's lettering and the board's slate still read, each on what holds it up.
3. **Given** a chunk was eaten, **When** 20 s pass with no bite on that piece, **Then** the earliest eaten chunk grows back over 400 ms (or appears at once under reduced motion), and the next regrows 20 s after, until the piece is whole; a whole piece shows its original meshes again, each as visible as it was before the first bite.
4. **Given** the desk's top carries the monitor, the signs, the laptop's stand, the keyboard, the mouse, the notebook, and the charger's desk poses, and the laptop's port carries the plugged charger, **When** every chunk is eaten, **Then** nothing floats: each live face stays on what holds it up, each glow card on what holds it, and each mounted piece on the set-aside part under it.

### User Story 3 - Degraded Modes Remove the Eating and Nothing Else (Priority: P1)

As a visitor on any device, I want the room to work as it did, whatever the eating can't do.

**Why this priority**: Principle II (production-grade or not shipped): the degraded mode ships with the happy path; and CLAUDE.md's degraded-mode rule for a new feature.

**Independent Test**: `vitest run` on the degraded-mode test: in each degraded state (the 2D room; the dinosaur's model absent or failed; a piece on its stand-in; reduced motion; paused room), read the "Eat" tile, the key, and the room's other behaviour.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the 2D room, **When** the Dinosaur is chosen, **Then** its flat figure shows as today, with no "Eat" tile and no Eat box (FR-012), and B does nothing.
2. **Given** the dinosaur has no model (spec 021's file absent or failed, so the flat fallback shows), **When** the figure walks, **Then** the eating is off: no tile, and B does nothing.
3. **Given** a piece shows its stand-in shape (its model failed, or is skipped on a weak device), **When** the figure reaches it, **Then** it isn't offered to eat.
4. **Given** reduced motion, **When** B is pressed at the desk, **Then** the chunk vanishes at once, with no flight, and one polite line is said (Q6).
5. **Given** the room is paused (spec 002, FR-060), **When** B is pressed, **Then** nothing happens, and a regrowth timer in progress waits.

### Edge Cases

- B pressed again while a chunk is in flight: ignored until it lands (one bite at a time).
- A piece is selected, or targeted, while a chunk of it is in flight: the border (spec 008) follows the meshes that stand; the highlight is refreshed at each bite and regrowth.
- The visitor leaves walking (Escape) during a flight: the chunk lands at once.
- The figure walks during a flight: the chunk flies to the mouth's point at each frame, so it follows the dinosaur.
- The visitor changes avatar: not in the room today (the choice is made at the door); if a later spec adds it, the eaten state stays with the pieces, not the avatar.
- The model of a piece swaps in after the first bite was impossible (it wasn't a model yet): the chunker runs on the model only, when it shows.
- The charger's pose changes with the tier (spec 004's switch `laptop-power`): every pose mounted on an edible piece sets its footprint aside, whichever shows now, so a later pose never lands on an eaten chunk; the charge light's glow card, which the pose turns on and off, stays on what holds it either way.
- A live face's own mesh is hidden by the loader (`scene/loader.ts`'s `prepareModel`; the room draws the face's quad over it, or for the laptop's screen nothing, spec 004's FR-031): its set-aside mesh stays hidden, and a whole piece gives each original mesh back the visibility it had (FR-004, FR-007).
- A phone with a low texture cap: chunk meshes reuse the piece's materials and textures, so the texture ledger (spec 004, FR-053) counts the same bytes.
- Two bites in the same frame from two inputs: one.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: The eating MUST be offered only in the 3D walking view, only while the visitor's figure is the dinosaur and shows its model, only on an edible piece the figure has reached (spec 008's reach), and only when the piece shows its model and one of its chunks stands that FR-005 lets it eat (FR-004, FR-005).
- **FR-002**: Which pieces are edible, and into how many chunks, MUST be room data, an `edible` key on a placement in `apps/web/room/scene.yaml` (`edible: { chunks: 6 }`), checked by the compositor (`build/room/schema.ts`): a count from 2 to 12, on a placement that has a `piece`, and never on `room-shell`. The starting data names `work-desk` (6), `busy-sign` (4), `laptop` (4), and `chalkboard` (4).
- **FR-003**: At the first bite on a piece, the room MUST first set aside the piece's fixed part (FR-004), then split the rest of its model into the placement's chunk count, by shape: the remaining triangles of every primitive of every mesh, taken together in the piece's own space, grouped by the nearest of N seed points spread over the rest's bounds (a k-means pass of a fixed iteration count over the triangles' centres, seeded by the piece's ID so the split is the same on every device and every visit). Each chunk MUST be built per primitive: one mesh for each primitive that has triangles in the chunk, carrying every vertex attribute the primitive has (position, normal, texture coordinates, and any other), and sharing that primitive's material, with no texture added. The set-aside part is built the same way, one mesh per primitive it holds. The result is memoized per piece.
- **FR-004**: The set-aside part MUST be, and MUST be only: (a) every mesh that carries a live face, the mesh named for a `live_faces` role in the piece's catalog entry (`apps/web/room/catalog/<piece>.yaml`: the busy sign's `lettering`, the chalkboard's `slate`, the laptop's `screen`); (b) every triangle that holds a face up: it overlaps the face's rectangle (its `at`, `size`, `up`, and `facing`) in the face's plane and lies wholly behind that plane (within 0.5 mm), to the piece's far side; (c) every triangle that carries a mount: it overlaps the footprint of a placement, or of a switch's pose, whose `mount.on` is this piece, that footprint being the mounted piece's catalog bounds as the mount places them, grown 10 mm in every direction (the desk's top under what stands on it; the laptop's `port` under the plugged charger); (d) every triangle that holds a glow card (the piece's catalog `glows`, as `scene/glows.ts` draws them): the mesh named for a glow's `id` (the laptop's `charge-light`), and every triangle that comes within the glow's `radius` of its `at` (for a path glow, which none of the four pieces has, within its `spread` of any of its points), except for a glow whose `at` lies on a live face (within 5 mm in front of the face's rectangle, inside it: the laptop's `screen-glow`), which stands on that face and its holders, set aside by (a) and (b) (Q119, Q-R2b). Every set-aside and chunk mesh MUST keep its source node's visibility: a live face's mesh, which `scene/loader.ts` hides (the room draws the face's quad under the placement, or for the laptop's unbound screen nothing), stays hidden in the set-aside part. The set-aside part is never eaten and never moves; nothing that stood on it or was held by it floats.
- **FR-005**: The set-aside part of every piece MUST always stand; a piece whose set-aside part is empty (none of the four starting pieces; a piece added later by configuration) MUST keep its last standing chunk. A piece's pick area, its frame box, its label, its topic, its selection, and its place in the "In the room" list MUST NOT change by eating.
- **FR-006**: A cut face (a chunk's boundary created by the split) MUST be a flat colour per material: for each primitive's material, its base colour darkened by 30%, unlit (a `MeshBasicMaterial`, drawn on the back side), shared by every chunk mesh of every primitive with that material, so no new texture or lighting cost.
- **FR-007**: A bite MUST be the nearest standing chunk to the dinosaur's mouth (A1), ties going to the lowest index; the eaten chunk flies to the mouth over at most 600 ms on a fixed ease and is hidden at its end. When the last eaten chunk of a piece has grown back, the piece MUST show its original meshes again, each with the visibility it had before the first bite (a live face's mesh stays hidden), and take its chunk and set-aside meshes out of the scene; nothing is disposed then (the chunks stay memoized for the next bite), and their geometry is disposed only with the stage. Only one bite at a time.
- **FR-008**: The 3D highlight (spec 008) MUST be refreshed at each bite's end and each regrowth, so a border follows the meshes that stand.
- **FR-009**: The eating MUST NOT run, or schedule anything, while the room is paused, while the loader shows, or after the room has fallen to 2D.
- **FR-010**: Under reduced motion (spec 002's `reducedMotion`, read throughout the visit), the flight and the regrowth's growth MUST be replaced by an immediate hide and an immediate return. ### Key Entities

- **Edible placement**: a placement with `edible.chunks`, from `scene.yaml`, validated at the build.
- **Set-aside part**: per piece, the face meshes and the triangles holding a face up, carrying a mount, or holding a glow (FR-004), one mesh per primitive, fixed, each as visible as its source.
- **Chunk**: one of the N groups of the rest, built as one mesh (and one cut-face mesh) per primitive it holds, with an index, a state (standing, flying, eaten, regrowing), and the time it was eaten.
- **Meal ledger**: per piece, the chunks' states and the regrowth order, in memory (FR-016); per visit, whether a piece's first bite has been said (FR-010).
- **Mouth**: the dinosaur figure's point for a bite (A1).
- **Eat box**: the "Eat" tile's place among the HUD's boxes, beside the walking control's, for a 3D dinosaur visit only (FR-012). It is reserved in the frame area's compact boxes and at spec 005's frame, and left out of the split layout's cover rule.
- **Dinosaur visit's stages**: the compositor's second sweep of the supported screens, with the Eat box reserved, in 3D. RG5, RG6, RG7, and the report judge it as they judge the plain sweep (FR-012; Q150, Q-R3a).
