## 1. Summary and branch

A visitor who chose the Dinosaur eats chunks of four pieces. The split is code, computed at the first bite from the piece's own model, after a fixed part is set aside (the live faces, what holds them up, what carries a mount, what holds a glow); what is edible is room data. ## 5. Design

### 5.1 Data (FR-002, FR-011)
`apps/web/room/scene.yaml`: `edible: { chunks: N }` on `work-desk` (6), `busy-sign` (4), `laptop` (4), `chalkboard` (4); a top-level `eating: { regrow_s: 20 }`. `build/room/schema.ts` takes both as strict keys (a `chunks` int 2 to 12; `regrow_s` 5 to 120); `build/room/compose.ts` carries them into the bundle (`compose/bundle.ts` types: `edible: { chunks } | null` on each placement, `eating: { regrow_s }` on the scene); a check (a new file in `build/room/checks/`) refuses `edible` on `room-shell` and on a placement with no `piece`. The one key is `eating.regrow_s` everywhere (N-2). Generated files regenerate with `npm run room:generate -w apps/web`.

The faces, glows, and mounts the chunker needs are already in the 3D bundle (`RoomScene`): `pieces[p].faces` (each live face's `at`, `size`, `facing`, `up`, in the piece's frame at scale 1), `pieces[p].glows` (each glow's `at` and `radius`, or its `path`), `pieces[p].anchors`, `pieces[p].scale`, and each placement's and switch pose's `mount`, `offset`, `turn`, and `model.bounds`. No build change carries them.

### 5.2 The chunker (FR-003, FR-004, FR-006), pure
`src/room/eat/chunker.ts`, Three.js-free, so it tests in Node:

- **Input**: the piece's primitives, `{ mesh: string, positions: Float32Array, index: Uint32Array | Uint16Array | null }[]` (one entry per primitive of every mesh, in the piece's own frame at scale 1). `mesh` carries the glTF mesh's name, the name (a) and (d) match against a face's role and a glow's `id`. For a one-primitive mesh it is the three.js mesh's own name. For a multi-primitive mesh, GLTFLoader makes a group carrying the name and children it names `<mesh>_<i>`, so `eat3d.ts` reads the name from the parent group, never the child's (N-2). T004 pins both cases, and the real laptop's `base` (4 primitives) and `charge-light` (1); its faces `{ role, at, size, facing, up }[]`; its glows `{ id, at, radius }[]` (a path glow, which none of the four pieces has, gives its points, each with its `spread` as the radius); its keep-out boxes (piece frame); `n`; `seed` (the piece's ID).
- **`setAside(input)`** → per primitive, the triangle indices of the set-aside part (FR-004): (a) every triangle of a primitive whose mesh is named for a face's role; (b) every triangle that overlaps a face's rectangle in the face's plane (its vertices projected on `right = up × facing` and `up`, against `size / 2`) and lies wholly behind it (every vertex's depth along `facing` at most +0.5 mm), to the piece's far side; (c) every triangle whose box overlaps a keep-out box; (d) every triangle of a primitive whose mesh is named for a glow's `id`, and, for each glow whose `at` isn't on a face (more than 5 mm in front of every face's plane, or outside its rectangle), every triangle whose closest point to `at` is within `radius`. Measured on 2026-11-14 on the laptop's file: the screen glow's `at` is 2.4 mm in front of the `screen` rectangle's centre, so it stands on the face (a, b); the charge light's ball takes 15 triangles (13 of `base`, the `charge-light` quad's 2); a ball at the screen glow's 0.18 m would take 20,419 of 31,016, which is why (d) excepts a glow on a face. Test helper: the set-aside count per piece, measured at busy sign 6, chalkboard 4, laptop 230 plus at most 15, desk 233 triangles; the test asserts the faces', holders', and glows' presence, not those counts.
- **Keep-out boxes** (built by the 3D side, §5.4, passed in): for each placement and each switch pose whose `mount.on` is this piece, the mounted model's `bounds` (its stand-in's box where it has no model), scaled by its piece's `scale`, turned and offset as its mount places it at the anchor, into this piece's frame, grown by `MOUNT_MARGIN = 0.01` m in every direction (NB-2). The desk's: the monitor, the laptop stand, both signs, the keyboard, the mouse, the notebook, and the charger's two desk poses; the laptop's: the charger's plugged pose at `port`.
- **`split(input, asideMask)`** → `{ chunks: number[][][] }`: per chunk, per primitive, its triangle indices. Seed points by a seeded PRNG from the piece's ID, spread over the rest's bounds; k-means over the remaining triangles' centres (all primitives together), a fixed 8 iterations; empty groups refilled from the largest; deterministic; every triangle in exactly one of the set-aside part and the N chunks.

### 5.3 The meal ledger (FR-005, FR-007, FR-011, FR-009, FR-010, FR-016), pure
`src/room/eat/meal.ts`: per piece, chunk states (`standing | flying | eaten | regrowing`), a `bite(piece, mouth)` that picks the nearest standing chunk (with an empty set-aside part, never the last standing one; one bite at a time), `tick(now)` that regrows the earliest eaten one after `eating.regrow_s`, a pause that stops the timer; and per piece, `firstBite(piece)`, true once a visit, which the polite line reads (FR-010). It sends nothing. It takes a clock (the room's `clock.ts`), so the tests use a fake one.

### 5.4 The 3D side (FR-003, FR-004, FR-006 to FR-008, FR-010, FR-015)
`src/room/eat/eat3d.ts` (3D chunk). At the first bite on a piece it reads the piece's model meshes, builds the keep-out boxes (§5.2), calls the chunker, and builds:

- **per chunk, per primitive** with triangles in it: a `BufferGeometry` with every attribute of the primitive (position, normal, uv, and any other), indexed by the chunk's triangles, a mesh sharing the primitive's material, and a cut-face mesh sharing the same geometry with the material's cut material;
- **the cut materials**: one per original material, a `MeshBasicMaterial` of its base colour × 0.7, `side: BackSide`, no texture (FR-006); the back faces of an open chunk show it, the back-face colour trick: no capping geometry, which is what the Author's look approval judges;
- **the set-aside part**: one mesh per primitive it holds, sharing the material, fixed.

**Visibility (RB-1)**: `scene/loader.ts`'s `prepareModel` hides each node named for a live face's role (`if (roles.has(node.name)) node.visible = false`): the room draws the face's quad under the placement (`scene/faces3d.ts`), and for the laptop's screen, which the scene binds to nothing (spec 004's FR-031), no quad at all, only the screen glow's card (`scene/glows.ts`). Each set-aside and chunk mesh takes its source's *effective* visibility (the source node and its ancestors up to the model's root), so the `lettering`, `slate`, and `screen` meshes stay hidden in the set-aside part. At the split, the piece records each original mesh's own `visible` and hides them (kept, not disposed); when the last eaten chunk has regrown, it restores each recorded flag, not `true`, and its chunk and set-aside meshes leave the scene, memoized, not disposed (N-3); the stage's own dispose frees them. The glow cards and the face quads hang under the placement's node, not the model, so the split never moves them; FR-004 (b) and (d) keep what holds them.

A bite flies one chunk (both its meshes) by a fixed ease over 600 ms to the mouth's point at each frame, hidden at its end; regrowth by scale 0 to 1 over 400 ms; reduced motion: at once. It calls `highlight.refresh()` at each end. Wiring: `scene.ts` constructs it when the stage has the dinosaur's model and the bundle has an `edible` placement.

**Draw calls (FR-015)**: a split piece draws `2 × (chunk–primitive meshes) + (set-aside meshes)`, a hidden mesh drawing nothing. Whole, the pieces draw busy sign 3 meshes (2 glTF meshes, 3 primitives), chalkboard 10 (2, 10), laptop 13 (6 glTF meshes, `base` 4 primitives, `lid` 4, `keyboard` 2, `charge-light`, `screen`, `trackpad` 1 each), desk 16 (3, 16). Measured on 2026-11-14 with a throwaway split of the four GLBs (the same set-aside without (d), and k-means, outside the repository): busy sign 14 split, chalkboard 44, laptop 68, desk 95 to 97; all four split at once, about 181 more. (d) adds to the laptop at most one set-aside mesh (the `charge-light` primitive's) and the `base` primitive's share it already has. FR-015's ceilings (100 a piece, 240 for all four) leave room for the implementation's own seeding; T008 asserts them on the real models.

## 8. Risks

- **The back-face colour trick may look wrong** on a thin piece (the busy sign). Mitigation: the Author's look approval (A4); the cut colour and the trick are one function, replaceable.
- **The set-aside part may look odd** (the desk's top patches under the mounted pieces standing over eaten legs; the sign's lettering on its back plate; the base's corner round the charge light). It is what keeps the faces, the glows, and the mounts from floating (FR-004); the Author's look approval judges it (A4), and a smaller set is a later look change, not a data loss.
- **Spec 021's names differ in its code.** One constant (§3), T000 reads them.
- **The draw calls of a split piece** (§5.4): about 181 more with all four split at once, on a phone. Mitigation: FR-015's ceilings, asserted on the real models (T008, T014); a whole piece goes back to its own meshes (FR-007); if a phone's frame time says so, the cut-face meshes are the first to go (the cut faces then show open), a look change for the Author.
- **Triangle counts of the studio models** (desk 37,796, laptop 31,016, board 6,034, sign 1,014): the split's budget (FR-015) is measured in `eat-chunker.test.ts` on the pieces' real files read from `assets/room/*.glb` at test time; the throwaway measurement ran the same passes, unoptimized, in CPython in under a second for each piece, so 50 ms in V8 has room, and the plan falls to a sampled k-means if over. (d)'s closest-point test runs only on glows not on a face (the laptop's charge light, 6 mm), behind a box test.
- **The face rule's depth**: FR-004 (b) takes the column behind a face to the piece's far side, not a few millimetres (spec, Revision 2026-11-14): on the sign, what holds the lettering lies 11 to 63 mm behind it. T004 asserts each face's holders on the real files.
- **The Eat box moves a dinosaur visit's framing** a little, for the whole visit (Q-R2a): the stages keep the selectables clear of one more 8 × 2.75 rem box. Every other visit is unchanged (SC-004). At spec 005's frame, the Eat box may cover a side-wall core, or a frame area may ease to a place where RG6 or RG7 finds a problem that a plain visit's doesn't. Then the room data doesn't build.
